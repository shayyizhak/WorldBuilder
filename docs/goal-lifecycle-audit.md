# Goal lifecycle audit — §1 of `brief-goalbook-into-the-fold.md`

> **Superseded in part by `docs/goalbook-phase-2-report.md`.** The findings stand; three of the numbers
> below do not.
>
> **Endings were 477, of which 15 were not endings.** `GoalBook.Remove` notified its watcher whether or

>
> Everything else here — the transition table, the 24-of-27 fold result, the silent/inferable/folded
> split, the six reducer sites, `TakePlace` having no creator, the 441 refusals — is unaffected.

**Status: HALT, as the brief requires.** §1 says to audit before emitting anything and to stop with
the transition table, because the emission design follows from it and should not be guessed at.
Nothing in §2, §3 or §4 has been built. No event kind has been added, no ruleset counter has moved,

### Creation — one phase, no event, eight sites

`PerceptionPhase.cs:37, 45, 60, 70, 94` (factions) and `:194, 236, 251` (actors). The phase's own
summary states the design plainly: *"This phase emits no events at all, which is the point."* That
was a claim about log noise, and it is also the whole defect. A goal carries the id of the event
that produced it, so causality survives — but the goal itself does not.

One creation site draws from the RNG stream: `PerceptionPhase.cs:219`, `RngPurpose.GoalFormation`,
the ambition spark. **This matters for §3.1** and is the one thing in the brief's risk model that is
already answerable: streams are derived per `(seed, year, entity, purpose)` with no shared
sequential generator, so emitting events consumes nothing that the spark roll is consuming. §3.1's
failure branch — "emission is drawing from the RNG stream" — is avoidable by construction rather
than by luck, provided emission takes no draw of its own.

### Mutation — progress only

`Goal.Target` is now `init`-only and the tree compiles. **Nothing retargets a goal**, and that is the
compiler saying so rather than a grep. `Goal.Arc` is written twice (`ActionPhase.cs:663, 906`), when
a goal spawns a war or a plot. There is no priority field. Progress moves at eleven sites and is the
only quantity that moves repeatedly.

### Ending — fifteen distinct reasons

The six in `EventReducer` (`:196, 234, 255, 286, 318, 490`) are inside the fold: death, exile,
defection, secession, partition, and the `disown` delta. **The brief and `StandingState` both say the
reducer touches goals "at exactly one point — a `disown` key". It is six, and five of them are
structural cases rather than payload keys.** `StandingState`'s comment has been corrected.

The nineteen in the rules are not folded. Eight of them sit beside an event that has just been
emitted and can point at it (`ActionPhase.cs:553, 785, 941, 995, 1026, 1087`; `ResolutionPhase.cs:400,
542`); the other eleven leave nothing at all (`ActionPhase.cs:242, 535, 716, 717, 809, 812, 947, 950`;
`ConsequencePhase.cs:810, 811, 812`).

## 4. What replay fails to reproduce — §3.2, answered early

Every component of `WorldState`, field at a time, 27 components, folded from each world's own board
and log:

| seed | checked | differ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 27 | `goals.arc`, `goals.identity`, `goals.progress` |
| 7 | 27 | `goals.arc`, `goals.identity`, `goals.progress` |
| 42 | 27 | `goals.arc`, `goals.identity`, `goals.progress` |
| 1234 | 27 | `goals.arc`, `goals.identity`, `goals.progress` |
| 2025 | 27 | `goals.arc`, `goals.identity`, `goals.progress` |

**Goals were the exception. The assertion passes everywhere else, on every seed.** So §3.2's cheap
standing protection is available now, and it has been taken: `ReplayTests` asserts the full 27-field
fingerprint and a second theory asserts that *exactly* these three components differ and no others.
That fails if a fourth ever starts diverging, and it fails equally loudly when these three stop —
which is how the phase-2 repair announces itself instead of needing to be remembered.

**A finding in its own right.** `ReplayTests.Fingerprint` was hand-written and read actors, places,
factions, arcs and relation *values* — not goals, and also not traits, yields, cells, succession
rules, seats, arc sides, or a relation's provenance. The test that exists to hold "state is a fold
over the log" could not fail on two thirds of the state. Same family as Layer 4 reading
`took`/`haul`/`plunder` while the engine wrote `loot`; ninth instance.

## 5. Three defects found in the instrument, before it was believed

Recorded because the memory that matters here is that every new instrument mis-fires on first
contact.

1. **A column that could only read zero.** The obvious way to measure "is this ending in the fold" is
   to fold the log and count. It reads 0 for all fifteen endings on all five seeds — not because the
   reducer cannot perform them, but because creation is not folded either, so there is nothing to
   remove. That is an unfalsifiable detector of the kind §6 of the project reference already names,
   and it nearly shipped as "no ending is in the fold". Replaced by `GoalAudit.ReducerReach`, which
   replays the real record with the book topped up between events so the column can vary. It now
   reads 378, and separates five reducer-owned endings from ten that are not.

2. **A label with no site.** The first draft of `GoalStep` carried `AssassinSent`. No site uses it —
   an assassination removes a goal on a kill and advances nothing — so the audit would have reported
   a structural zero it had invented. Removed. A label with no emitter is worse than a dead branch,
   including inside the instrument.

3. **A branch hidden by a shared label.** `RetireGoals` collapsed three reasons into one `if`. Split
   out, the dead-owner branch reads **0**: the reducer clears an actor's goals on the death event,
   which lands earlier in the same year, so that branch cannot be the one that catches a dead owner.
   While the two shared a label the panel's 27 clears made both look live. Same shape as the
   covert-coup `seized`/`exposed` pair.

## 6. Findings for §2 to answer

Ranked by how much they change the emission design.

1. **Creation is the whole defect; removal is half-fixed already.** Six of twenty-five endings fold.
   Emitting creation alone would make replay reproduce goals whose endings are then applied by the
   reducer for five of the fifteen reasons and by nothing for the other ten. **The record needs
   creation and the ten unfolded endings, or it needs neither.** A partial fix leaves a world whose
   goal book fills and never empties — strictly worse than one that stays empty.

2. **189 endings of 477 leave no trace at all.** `Expired` (80) and `Completed` (74) are the bulk.
   Both are the retirement sweep, both are the ordinary way a goal finishes, and neither is
   inferable from anything.

3. **235 endings are inferable but unnamed.** `Achieved` and `Spent` sit beside a conquest, an
   alliance, a coup, a betrayal, a homecoming or a death. §2 has to decide whether those get their own
   event or ride the existing one as a payload key — the `RelationEnds.OnItsOwnEvent` versus
   `RelationEnds.Into` distinction, already built and already argued. **Recommendation: `Into`.** A
   goal ending beside the event that ended it is the collapse case, not the trade-collapse case, and
   158 + 77 extra events would roughly double the panel's log for transitions no reader asked about.

4. **`GoalKind.TakePlace` is created by nothing.** `ActionPhase.cs:46` handles it beside `ControlOre`
   and no rule adds one. A goal kind with no creator, same family as the four declared-and-never-
   emitted event kinds. Not this brief's job to fix, and it should be carded.

5. **441 refusals against 505 creations.** The book turns away nearly as much intent as it admits,
   almost all `BookFull` against `MaxPerOwner = 2`. No counter saw this before. It is not a defect —
   the cap is deliberate — but it is a large, previously invisible number sitting on the mechanic §2
   is about to make recordable, and it bears on whether a refusal is worth recording too.

6. **`OwnerLeftFaction`, `TargetInvalid` and `OwnerDeadAtRetirement` are never reached on this
   panel.** Three rules-layer sites with no exercise. Reported as unexercised, not as unreachable —
   the distinction this project has already paid for confusing, and only reading the rules settles it.
