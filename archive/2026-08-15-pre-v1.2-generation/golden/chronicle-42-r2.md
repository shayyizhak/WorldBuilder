## Wars

### The War for Threi Cut: the Griwick Compact against the Wurn League, 5–8

The Griwick Compact declared war on the Wurn League in year 5 over Threi Cut. The Wurn League defeated the Griwick Compact at Threi Cut in year 5, with 45 dead. The Griwick Compact defeated the Wurn League at Threi Cut in year 6, with 38 dead.

In year 7, the Griwick Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Threi Cut, with 32 dead. The Griwick Compact took Threi Cut from the Wurn League in year 7. The Griwick Compact and the Wurn League made peace in year 8 after 3 years, both sides exhausted.

### The War for Threi Cut of 7: the Kebarrow Compact against the Wurn League, 7–9

The Kebarrow Compact declared war on the Wurn League in year 7 over Threi Cut. In that same year, the Kebarrow Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Laehiford, where 142 people died. The Kebarrow Compact took Laehiford from the Wurn League as a result of this victory.

In year 8, the Kebarrow Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Hadale, where 124 people died. The fighting continued into year 9.

In year 9, the Kebarrow Compact and the Wurn League made peace after two years of conflict. Both sides were exhausted.

### The Wurn League, 2–21

The Wurn League’s twenty-year existence, spanning years 2 to 21, began with territorial expansion and ended with total dissolution. It started by occupying Threi Cut in year 2, a site no one held, and secured its position through six marriages binding it to the Kebarrow Compact and the Griwick Compact. The period was defined by a persistent quarrel with the Griwick Compact, which resulted in three battles, and a series of defeats at the hands of the Kebarrow Compact that stripped the League of its lands. Three people held the seat during this time: Reweld Wul, Math Ham, and Trem Lolkoll. All three were cast out, and the League itself was finished in year 20, leaving its last 21 followers to scatter.

Reweld Wul held the seat from the start of the period until year 7. His rule ended after an attempt on the life of Searn Sisrill failed and was traced back to him. In the same year, Math Ham contested the claim of Witrou Breim, the named heir, and took the seat, setting aside Witrou Breim’s claim under the rules of primogeniture. Witrou Breim was cast out for the losing claim. Math Ham then had Searn Sisrill murdered at Kebarrow in year 8. Math Ham’s rule was marked by internal violence; he had Reweld Wul murdered at Kebarrow in year 15 and Thulgea Bu murdered at Kebarrow in year 16.

The League’s military standing deteriorated rapidly. It defeated the Griwick Compact at Threi Cut in year 5, killing 45, but lost to the Griwick Compact at Threi Cut in year 6 (38 dead) and at Threi Cut in year 7 (32 dead). In year 7, the Griwick Compact took Threi Cut from the League. That same year, the Kebarrow Compact defeated the League at Laehiford (142 dead) and took the settlement. The League lost Hadale to the Kebarrow Compact in year 8 (124 dead) and again in year 20 (106 dead). The League sent out five raids: one carried off plunder from Griwick in year 6, while three got through but took nothing, and one was beaten off. It suffered five raids, three of which were beaten off.

Trem Lolkoll took the seat in year 17 by openly challenging Math Ham and winning. Math Ham was cast out for the losing side of the open challenge. Trem Lolkoll’s rule was short; in year 20, his attempt on Weallhous Dreld failed and was traced back. He was cast out for attempted murder. In that same year, the Kebarrow Compact declared war on the League over a long-standing grievance and took Hadale. The Wurn League was finished, its landless status confirmed as its last 21 followers scattered. The period ended with the League destroyed, having lost three places and fought six battles, winning only one.

### The Sworn Men of Meigate, 19–51

The Sworn Men of Meigate spent thirty-three years as a fractured power, beginning with a break from the Kebarrow Compact and ending in total destruction by the Vea Lode Covenant. Its history was defined by internal instability, repeated military failures, and a long-standing grievance that culminated in war. Three people held the seat during this period, each ruling for an average of ten years, but the power’s standing eroded through famine, failed succession, and external defeat.

Renbeir Surn took the seat in 19 when Meigate broke from the Kebarrow Compact. He faced an open challenge from Sou Dra in 22, which he won, keeping the seat while casting out the loser. Renbeir died in 25, and Kreathbeas Waeth took the seat. Under Kreathbeas, the Sworn Men sent eight raids: two against the Kebarrow Compact carried off plunder, while six against the Griwick Compact and later the Vea Lode Covenant were beaten off. The period saw three years of hunger at Meigate, killing 186 people and driving 238 to flee. Internal strife grew as eight exiles returned to service, yet Gatros Hearn attempted to murder Kreathbeas in 41 and was cast out.

Kreathbeas died in 48, and Tor Nathgoull took the seat by setting aside the named heir Herpeim Raern. The Vea Lode Covenant declared war over a long-standing grievance and defeated the Sworn Men at Meigate in 49 and 50, killing 149 and 124 respectively. The Covenant took Meigate in 50, and the Sworn Men of Meigate was finished, its last 12 followers scattering. The power had lost one place and fought two battles, losing both. Peace was made in 51, but the house had ended.

### The Sworn Men of Laehiford, 20–51

The Sworn Men of Laehiford spent thirty-two years as an independent power, defined by a cycle of internal instability and external raiding. The period began with a break from the Kebarrow Compact and ended with the faction still holding its seat, though its leadership had shifted twice. The group’s standing was marked by frequent purges and the return of exiles, suggesting a volatile internal order that relied on election to resolve succession disputes.

Realsis Leirpu held the seat from 20 until 32. His rule ended after an attempt on Thold Valmaer failed in 32, leading to his casting out for attempted murder. Math Ham then contested the claim of Sou Dra, the named heir, in an election. Math Ham won, taking the seat in 32 and setting aside Sou Dra’s claim, who was subsequently cast out. Math Ham ruled until his death in 39. During his tenure, the Sworn Men raided Kebarrow in 23, carrying off 31 grain and killing 23, and raided Threi Cut in 31, taking 34 ore and killing 2.

Heth Fal took the seat in 39 after contesting the claim of Herpeim Raern, the named heir, in an election. Heth Fal won, setting aside Herpeim’s claim and casting him out. Heth Fal remained in power through the end of the period. Under his rule, the Sworn Men’s raids were largely unsuccessful; attempts on Vea Lode in 29 and 43, Threi Cut in 44, and Griwick in 33 and 47 were all beaten off. The faction suffered a plague at Laehiford in 33 that killed 68 people. Heth Fal ordered the murder of Thosruld Lul at Hadale in 43. The period ended with Heth Fal still holding the seat.

### The Kebarrow Compact, 22–41

The Kebarrow Compact endured twenty years of internal fragmentation and external hostility, defined by a succession of eleven rulers who held the seat for an average of 1.7 years each. The period began with Weallhous Dreld holding power, bound by marriage to the Sworn Men of Meigate and the Sworn Men of Laehiford, but ended with Paernmel Has still in the seat after a decade of violent turnover. The Compact’s stability was undermined by a pattern of twenty individuals being courted away from its ruler, a structural decay that facilitated frequent purges and contested successions. While the Compact fought three wars against the Griwick Compact, winning all three battles at Kebarrow, it suffered the loss of Hadale to the Hadale Commune and endured two raids that carried off plunder.

The early years were marked by the consolidation of alliances and the violent removal of challengers. Weallhous Dreld kept the seat after defeating Saern Meastouth’s open challenge in 23, but was himself killed by Gatros Hearn in 25 after losing an open challenge. Gatros Hearn’s rule was brief; he was cast out after losing a subsequent challenge to Teillmol Lund in 27. Teillmol Lund was also cast out in 28 for attempted murder, leading to the election of Theald Va. Theald Va was murdered in 29 by Wilwound Ska, who then took the seat by setting aside the claim of the named heir Beas Krouthea. Wilwound Ska was killed in 31 by Stonand Ker, who had been motivated by the earlier raid on Threi Cut. Le Vild took the seat in 31 by setting aside the claim of Kou Peis, but was cast out in 33 for attempted murder.

Heth Fal took the seat in 33 by setting aside the claim of Drarka Draernthun, but his rule was short-lived. He was cast out in 35 for attempted murder, and Nael War took the seat by election. Nael War was murdered in 37 by Neildvarn Tramern, leading to the election of Paernrom Sir. Paernrom Sir was murdered in 38 by Throll Kell, and Kondruth Tru took the seat by election. Kondruth Tru was cast out in 39 after losing an open challenge to Paernmel Has, who held the seat at the end of the period. Throughout these years, the Compact fought the Griwick Compact in three battles at Kebarrow in 32, 33, and 34, defeating them each time with 103, 121, and 60 dead respectively. The war ended in 35 with peace, leaving both sides exhausted.

### The Hadale Commune, 27–51

The period saw three rulers hold the seat for an average of eight years each. The commune sent four raids, all beaten off, and suffered one plague that killed 86 people. Its internal stability was fragile, marked by four cast-outs and two outlaws declared against those who had already left.

Sothkel Sald held the seat until 38, when Sou Dra challenged him openly, won, and took the seat. Sou Dra killed Sothkel Sald at Hadale that same year. Sou Dra’s rule lasted nine years, ending in 47 when Durnrin Drar had him murdered at Hadale. Durnrin Drar took the seat by election. During this time, four exiles returned to serve the commune: Kou Peis in 32, Sou Dra in 34, Realsis Leirpu in 35, and Thosruld Lul in 38. The period ended in 51 with Durnrin Drar still holding the seat.

### The Kebarrow Compact, 42–51

The Kebarrow Compact endured a decade of internal collapse and external failure, beginning with Paernmel Has holding the seat and ending with Wuldweald Valdrith in possession after a violent succession. The period was defined by a succession of failed attempts on Paernmel Has’s life, which drove four men out of the Compact and left three others declared outlaws. Simultaneously, the Compact’s military efforts against the Vea Lode Covenant failed completely, with all three raids beaten off. The ruler’s authority eroded through the murder of his own people and the eventual assassination of Paernmel Has himself, which triggered a contested election that Wuldweald Valdrith won.

Paernmel Has maintained the seat from the start of the period until his death in 51. His rule was marked by repeated attempts on his life by Stonand Ker in 43, Keithfal Naell in 45, Throll Kell in 46, and Drouldthas Stour in 49. Each attempt failed and was traced back, resulting in the cast-out of all four men for attempted murder. Their earlier conspiracies were uncovered in 46, leading to their declaration as outlaws. Paernmel Has responded to the instability by ordering the murder of Veillpea Dourn at Vea Lode in 46 and Thres Thrild at Griwick in 47. He also secured alliances through marriage, binding the Compact to the Sworn Men of Laehiford in 48 via the unions of Paendoull Maedrael and Wea Thrandgo, and Brodall Rous and Baedros Mam.

The Compact’s external posture was uniformly unsuccessful. It sent three raids against the Vea Lode Covenant, targeting Vea Lode in 43, Threi Cut in 48, and Griwick in 49; all three were beaten off. Internal cohesion fractured further as Throll Kell courted away three people from Paernmel Has between 42 and 44. The period ended with the murder of Paernmel Has by Wuldweald Valdrith at Kebarrow in 51. Wuldweald Valdrith contested the claim of the named heir Hehum Skul in an election, took the seat, and cast out Hehum Skul, who had already lost his claim. Wuldweald Valdrith held the seat at the end of the period.
