# A history of the Rethkeis Vale

Seed 42, years 1–51. Every name, date and figure below is drawn from the simulation itself.


## Wars

_In the order they begin._

### The War for Threi Cut: the Griwick Compact against the Wurn League, 5–8

The Griwick Compact declared war on the Wurn League in year 5 over Threi Cut. The Wurn League defeated the Griwick Compact at Threi Cut in year 5, with 45 dead. The Griwick Compact defeated the Wurn League at Threi Cut in year 6, with 38 dead.

In year 7, the Griwick Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Threi Cut, with 32 dead. The Griwick Compact took Threi Cut from the Wurn League in year 7. The Griwick Compact and the Wurn League made peace in year 8 after 3 years, both sides exhausted.

### The War for Threi Cut of 7: the Kebarrow Compact against the Wurn League, 7–9

The Kebarrow Compact declared war on the Wurn League in year 7 over Threi Cut. In that same year, the Kebarrow Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Laehiford, where 142 people died. The Kebarrow Compact took Laehiford from the Wurn League as a result of this victory.

In year 8, the Kebarrow Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Hadale, where 124 people died. The fighting continued into year 9.

In year 9, the Kebarrow Compact and the Wurn League made peace after two years of conflict. Both sides were exhausted.


## Powers

_In the order they begin._

### The Kebarrow Compact, 2–21

The Kebarrow Compact’s twenty-year period from 2 to 21 was defined by a succession of short, violent rulerships and a relentless, ultimately successful war against the Wurn League. The era began with the Compact bound to the Griwick Compact through two marriages in year 2. It ended with the Compact having destroyed the Wurn League, lost two major settlements to breakaway factions, and seen its own standing collapse to nothing in one of those places. Seven people held the seat during this time, five of them killed, with the average rule lasting 2.7 years.

The Compact’s early years were marked by mutual raiding with the Griwick Compact and the Wurn League. In year 4, the Compact raided Laehiford, carrying off 29 grain and killing 29, while the Griwick Compact raided Kebarrow, taking 43 grain and killing 28. The Wurn League raided Kebarrow and Meigate in year 5 and 6, taking nothing but killing 27 and 30 respectively. These attacks triggered failed Counter-raids by the Compact in year 5 and 6. In year 7, the Compact declared war on the Wurn League for Threi Cut. The Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Laehiford, killing 142, and took the settlement. They defeated the Wurn League again at Hadale in year 8, killing 124, before making peace after two years of war.

Internal instability plagued the Compact. Searn Sisrill was killed by Math Ham in year 8, and Reweld Wul took the seat by election. Reweld Wul was killed by Math Ham in year 15, and Witrou Breim took the seat by election. Thulgea Bu challenged Witrou Breim openly in year 16, won, and took the seat, then killed Witrou Breim. Thulgea Bu was killed by Math Ham later that year. Thra Bround contested the claim of the named heir Deargund Keirem, won by election, and took the seat, casting out Deargund Keirem. Nael War had Thra Bround murdered in year 18. Krir Nur contested the claim of the named heir Sou Dra, won by election, and took the seat, casting out Sou Dra. Weallhous Dreld challenged Krir Nur openly in year 20, won, and took the seat, casting out Krir Nur.

The Compact’s external position deteriorated despite its military victories. In year 19, Meigate rose against the Compact, whose standing had fallen to nothing, and broke away as the Sworn Men of Meigate with Renbeir Surn taking its seat. In year 20, the Compact declared war on the Wurn League over a long-standing grievance. The Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Hadale, killing 106, and took the settlement. The Wurn League was finished, its last 21 followers scattering. Laehiford broke from the Compact as the Sworn Men of Laehiford with Realsis Leirpu taking its seat. Hadale rose against the Compact, whose standing had fallen to nothing, in year 21. The Compact and the Wurn League made peace after one year, following the League's collapse.

### The Wurn League, 2–21

The Wurn League’s twenty-year existence, spanning years 2 to 21, began with territorial expansion and ended in total dissolution. It started by occupying Threi Cut in year 2, a site no one held, and secured its position through six marriages binding it to the Kebarrow Compact and the Griwick Compact. The period was defined by a persistent quarrel with the Griwick Compact over Threi Cut, which resulted in three battles. The League won one engagement in year 5 but lost the subsequent two in years 6 and 7, losing Threi Cut to the Griwick Compact in year 7.

Internal instability accelerated the League’s decline. Reweld Wul was cast out in year 7 for an attempted murder, allowing Math Ham to take the seat by setting aside the named heir’s claim. Math Ham’s rule was marked by further violence, including the murder of Searn Sisrill in year 8 and Reweld Wul in year 15. In year 17, Trem Lolkoll challenged Math Ham openly, won, and took the seat, casting out the loser. Trem Lolkoll’s own rule ended in year 20 when he was cast out for an attempted murder, triggering a war with the Kebarrow Compact.

The final phase saw the League lose all its holdings. The Kebarrow Compact defeated the League at Hadale in year 20 and took the settlement. The Wurn League was finished in year 20, landless, with its last 21 followers scattering. Over the period, the League fought six battles, winning one and losing five. It sent five raids, one of which carried off plunder, while suffering five raids itself. Three people held the seat, each ruling for an average of 6.0 years, and four people were cast out.

### The Griwick Compact, 4–23

The Griwick Compact’s rule from year 4 to 23 was defined by a cycle of external aggression, internal decay, and violent succession. The period began with the Compact raiding Kebarrow in year 4, an act triggered by famine at Vea Lode. This initial aggression was followed by a war against the Wurn League over Threi Cut, which the Compact lost in year 5 before winning it back in year 7. The power’s standing eroded steadily, leading to rebellions in Threi Cut in year 13 and Vea Lode in year 15, where its authority had fallen to nothing. The era concluded with a series of murders among its rulers, ending with Turaer Danpa taking the seat in year 23.

The Compact’s military engagements were limited but decisive. It fought three battles against the Wurn League, losing one at Threi Cut in year 5 with 45 dead, and winning two in years 6 and 7 with 38 and 32 dead respectively. It sent six raids, three of which carried off plunder from Kebarrow in years 4 and 17 and Laehiford in year 12, while three were beaten off at Hadale in years 7 and 22 and Meigate in year 13. It suffered three raids, one by the Wurn League at Griwick in year 6 and two by the Kebarrow Compact at Griwick in year 13 and Vea Lode in year 19, the latter two being beaten off. The war with the Wurn League lasted three years, ending in peace in year 8.

Internal stability collapsed as the Compact’s standing fell. Hunger killed 93 people across its territories over 13 years, with 201 fleeing their homes. The seat passed three times among four rulers. Pouldrir Ho, who held the seat since year 1, was killed in year 20. Heillvar Maer took the seat in year 20 after his claim was upheld, but was killed in year 21. Befu Seirn took the seat in year 21 by the strongest claim and was killed in year 23. Turaer Danpa took the seat in year 23 by the strongest claim and remained holding it at the end of the period. One person was cast out, and two exiles returned to service.

### The Sworn Men of Meigate, 19–51

The Sworn Men of Meigate held power for thirty-three years, from 19 to 51, before being destroyed by the Vea Lode Covenant. The period began with a break from the Kebarrow Compact and ended with the scattering of its last twelve followers. Three people held the seat, each ruling for an average of 10.3 years. The power started as an independent entity under Renbeir Surn, survived internal challenges and famines, and ended when its seat was taken and its land lost.

Renbeir Surn took the seat in 19 when Meigate broke from the Kebarrow Compact. He faced an open challenge from Sou Dra in 22, which he won, leading to Sou Dra’s exile. Renbeir died in 25, and Kreathbeas Waeth took the seat. Under Kreathbeas, the Sworn Men sent eight raids: two against the Kebarrow Compact in 20 and 23 carried off plunder, while six raids between 28 and 43 against the Griwick Compact and the Vea Lode Covenant were beaten off. The power suffered one raid from the Vea Lode Covenant in 43, which killed 22 but took nothing. Three famines struck Meigate in 26, 42, and 43, killing 186 people and causing 238 to flee.

Kreathbeas died in 48, and Tor Nathgoull took the seat by setting aside the claim of the named heir Herpeim Raern. Tor Nathgoull had Keithfal Naell murdered at Vea Lode in 50. The Vea Lode Covenant defeated the Sworn Men at Meigate in 49 and 50, killing 149 and 124 respectively. The Covenant took Meigate in 50, and the Sworn Men of Meigate was finished. The period ended in 51 with peace between the Covenant and the scattered remnants.

### The Sworn Men of Laehiford, 20–51

The Sworn Men of Laehiford spent thirty-two years as an independent power, defined by a pattern of returning exiles and failed aggression. The period began in 20 when Laehiford broke from the Kebarrow Compact, with Realsis Leirpu taking the seat. The power ended in 51 with Heth Fal still holding the seat, having ruled for twelve years. Three people held the seat during this time, each for an average of 10.3 years. The rule passed from one person to another only twice: first when Realsis Leirpu was cast out in 32, and second when Math Ham died in 39.

Realsis Leirpu’s rule was short and ended in disgrace. He took service with the power in 20, but his tenure was marked by a failed attempt on Thold Valmaer in 32. This act was traced back to him, and he was cast out for attempted murder. Math Ham succeeded him after contesting the claim of Sou Dra, the named heir, in an election. Math Ham set aside Sou Dra’s claim and took the seat in 32. Sou Dra was cast out for the losing claim. Math Ham ruled until his death in 39. Heth Fal then contested the claim of Herpeim Raern, the named heir, in an election. Heth Fal set aside Herpeim Raern’s claim and took the seat in 39. Herpeim Raern was cast out for the losing claim. Heth Fal remained in power through 51.

The power’s external relations were characterized by repeated raids and shifting alliances. It sent seven raids, five of which were beaten off and two of which carried off plunder. The successful raids were against Kebarrow in 23, carrying off 31 grain and killing 23, and against Threi Cut in 31, carrying off 34 ore and killing 2. The beaten-off raids targeted Vea Lode in 29, Griwick in 33, Vea Lode in 43, Threi Cut in 44, and Griwick in 47. The power ordered one killing against people of other powers: Thosruld Lul was murdered at Hadale in 43. No people of the Sworn Men were murdered from within.

Internal stability was maintained through the return of exiles. Fourteen people returned from exile and took service with the power between 22 and 51. These returns included Trem Lolkoll in 22, Math Ham in 24, Sou Dra in 24, Teillmol Lund in 31, Le Vild in 34, Drarka Draernthun in 35, Heth Fal in 37, Herpeim Raern in 39, Draes Wild in 43, Stonand Ker in 46, Voudreirn Wer in 46, Kou Peis in 47, Thurnean Kourn in 48, and Drouldthas Stour in 51. The power suffered one year of sickness in 33, when a plague at Laehiford killed 68 people.

Alliances shifted through marriage. Three marriages tied the power to others. In 26, Sou Dra married Wilwound Ska, binding the Sworn Men to the Kebarrow Compact. In 36, Drarka Draernthun married Baeld Fea, binding the power to the Hadale Commune. In 44, Draes Wild married Ror Rim, binding the power to the Kebarrow Compact. Despite these ties, the power remained distinct. Five people were courted away from its ruler in the final years: Voudreirn Wer won Baedros Mam away in 48, Teillmol Lund won Baedros Mam away in 49, Voudreirn Wer won Stonand Ker away in 49, Voudreirn Wer won Draes Wild away in 50, and Thurnean Kourn won Draes Wild away in 51.

### The Kebarrow Compact, 22–41

The Kebarrow Compact endured twenty years of internal fragmentation and external hostility, defined by a succession of eleven rulers who held the seat for an average of 1.7 years each. The period began with the Compact bound to the Sworn Men of Meigate and the Sworn Men of Laehiford through marriage, but these alliances were quickly tested by raids and open challenges. The Compact suffered three raids in its early years, two of which carried off plunder, and lost the settlement of Hadale to the Hadale Commune. Its standing was further eroded by a war with the Griwick Compact that lasted three years and resulted in three battles, all won by the Compact but leaving both sides exhausted.

Internal stability was nonexistent, characterized by a cycle of attempted murders, castings-out, and assassinations. Twelve people were cast out of the Compact during this period, six for attempted murder, four for losing a claim to the seat, and two for losing an open challenge. Six of the eleven rulers were killed, often by their own people, while five were cast out. The seat passed ten times, with rulers falling to assassination, open challenge, or exile. The Compact’s own raids on Threi Cut and Griwick were both beaten off, and it ordered two killings against people of other powers, yet it could not prevent the erosion of its territory or the constant turnover of its leadership.

By the end of the period, the Compact had survived the war with the Griwick Compact and maintained its core structure, but it was ruled by Paernmel Has, who had taken the seat in 39. The long-standing grievance with the Griwick Compact had been resolved by peace, but the Compact had lost Hadale and remained vulnerable to internal betrayal. The period ended with the Compact still holding the seat, but its history was marked by the high cost of its victories and the fragility of its internal order.

### The Griwick Compact, 24–36

The Griwick Compact’s rule from 24 to 36 was defined by rapid succession, internal decay, and total destruction. Four people held the seat in thirteen years, with an average tenure of 2.7 years and a median of 1.0. The period began with Turaer Danpa, who had held the seat since 23, and ended with the Compact finished and landless. Its standing fell to nothing, and its last 6 followers scattered. The Compact fought five battles and won none, while suffering ten raids and losing three places.

The seat passed three times through violence. In 24, Thold Valmaer had Turaer Danpa murdered at Griwick, and Leimmil Theall took the seat by the strongest claim. In 25, Gatros Hearn killed Leimmil Theall at Griwick, and Thold Valmaer took the seat by the strongest claim. Thold Valmaer ruled until 34, when he was killed by Heth Fal at Griwick. Veillpea Dourn contested the claim of Thurnean Kourn, the named heir, but Thurnean Kourn took the seat and Veillpea Dourn was cast out. Thurnean Kourn’s house ended in 35.

The Compact’s decline was marked by plague and war. A plague broke out at Griwick in 26, killing 185 people. In 27, 133 died and 296 fled. In 28, 156 died and 208 fled. The Compact declared war on the Kebarrow Compact in 32 over a long-standing grievance. The Kebarrow Compact and its allies defeated the Griwick Compact at Kebarrow in 32, 33, and 34, with 103, 121, and 60 dead respectively. The Vea Lode Covenant broke from the Compact in 29, taking Vea Lode. Threi Cut rose against the Compact in 31, and the Vea Lode Covenant took Threi Cut in 34. The Vea Lode Covenant declared war on the Compact in 34 and defeated it at Threi Cut and Griwick in 34 and 35, taking Griwick in 35. The Compact was finished in 35.

### The Hadale Commune, 27–51

The Hadale Commune existed for twenty-five years, from 27 to 51, as a breakaway power that struggled to maintain its sovereignty against internal dissent and external raids. It began when Hadale separated from the Kebarrow Compact, with Sothkel Sald taking the seat. The period saw three rulers hold the seat for an average of eight years each, with the seat passing between them only twice. The Commune’s authority was repeatedly challenged from within, resulting in four castings out and two declarations of outlawry against those who had already left. Its external posture was uniformly unsuccessful; all four raids it sent out were beaten off.

Sothkel Sald ruled from 27 until 38, when Sou Dra challenged him openly, won, and took the seat. Sothkel Sald was killed by Sou Dra at Hadale that same year. During this transition, four exiles returned to take service with the Commune: Kou Peis in 32, Sou Dra in 34, Realsis Leirpu in 35, and Thosruld Lul in 39. Sou Dra’s rule lasted nine years, ending in 47 when Durnrin Drar had him murdered at Hadale. Durnrin Drar took the seat by election. The period included one year of sickness at Hadale in 40, which killed 86 people.

Under Durnrin Drar, the Commune continued to face internal instability and external failure. Four people were courted away from its ruler during the period, a pattern indicating fragmentation. The Commune ordered one killing against a person of another power, Gatros Hearn at Vea Lode in 48. Its four raids—against Vea Lode in 42, Threi Cut in 43, Griwick in 45, and Meigate in 51—were all beaten off. The Commune remained in existence in 51 with Durnrin Drar still holding the seat.

### The Vea Lode Covenant, 29–48

The Vea Lode Covenant existed for twenty years, from 29 to 48, as a power defined by its separation from the Griwick Compact and its subsequent dominance over that same rival. It began when Vea Lode broke from the Griwick Compact in 29, with Stald Gearngoll taking the seat. The period opened with severe internal weakness, marked by famine that killed 9 people and left grain short by 4 in 30. This scarcity drove a raid on Griwick in 30 and another on Threi Cut in 32, both of which were beaten off. The Covenant’s standing improved through military success against its former overlord, taking Threi Cut in 34 and Griwick in 35, which finished the Griwick Compact. The period ended with the Covenant still holding its seat, but under a new ruler, Keithfal Naell, who took power in 48 after the previous ruler, Gatros Hearn, was killed.

The Covenant’s internal stability was fragile, characterized by frequent changes in leadership and recurrent famine. Five people held the seat during these twenty years, with an average rule of 3.8 years and a median of 1.0 year. The seat passed from one person to another four times inside this period. Stald Gearngoll, who took the seat in 29, was killed in 45. His death triggered a contest decided by election, which Veillpea Dourn won, setting aside the claim of the named heir Kou Peis. Veillpea Dourn was killed in 46, leading to Thres Thrild taking the seat by election. Thres Thrild was killed in 47, leading to Gatros Hearn taking the seat by election, also setting aside a named heir. Gatros Hearn was killed in 48, leading to Keithfal Naell taking the seat by election. Four of the five rulers were killed; one was still holding at the end of this period. Three people were cast out: two for the losing claim and one, Beas Krouthea, for conspiracy against the seat. Six exiles returned to take service with the Covenant.

The Covenant’s external relations were shaped by its wars with the Griwick Compact and its alliances with other powers. It fought two battles, both won: it beat the Griwick Compact at Threi Cut in 34, killing 13, and at Griwick in 35, killing 113. It began one war, which lasted two years. The Covenant sent three raids: two against the Griwick Compact in 30 and 32, both beaten off, and one against Meigate in 43, which got through but took nothing. It suffered eleven raids, all beaten off, from the Sworn Men of Meigate, the Hadale Commune, the Kebarrow Compact, and the Sworn Men of Laehiford. The Covenant bound itself to other powers through four marriages: to the Hadale Commune in 35 and 41, to the Kebarrow Compact in 39, and to the Sworn Men of Meigate in 41. Two people were courted away from its ruler. The period was also marked by hunger at Vea Lode for five years, killing 42 people in all, with 90 leaving their homes.

### The Kebarrow Compact, 42–51

The Kebarrow Compact endured a decade of internal collapse and external failure. Its rule passed from Paernmel Has to Wuldweald Valdrith, with Paernmel Has killed and Wuldweald Valdrith still holding the seat at the end of the period. The power sent out three raids, all of which were beaten off. Five people were cast out, and three were declared outlaws. The ruler’s authority eroded as three people were courted away from him.

Paernmel Has held the seat since 39. In 43, Stonand Ker attempted his life and was cast out. That same year, a raid on Vea Lode against the Vea Lode Covenant was beaten off. In 45, Keithfal Naell attempted Paernmel Has’s life and was cast out. In 46, Throll Kell attempted Paernmel Has’s life and was cast out. Paernmel Has ordered the murder of Veillpea Dourn at Vea Lode in 46. In 46, Stonand Ker, Keithfal Naell, and Throll Kell were all declared outlaws for conspiracies uncovered after their departures.

In 47, Paernmel Has ordered the murder of Thres Thrild at Griwick. In 48, marriages bound the Kebarrow Compact to the Sworn Men of Laehiford. That year, a raid on Threi Cut against the Vea Lode Covenant was beaten off. In 49, Drouldthas Stour attempted Paernmel Has’s life and was cast out. A raid on Griwick against the Vea Lode Covenant was beaten off. In 50, a marriage bound the Kebarrow Compact to the Hadale Commune. In 51, Wuldweald Valdrith murdered Paernmel Has at Kebarrow. Wuldweald Valdrith took the seat, setting aside the claim of the named heir Hehum Skul, who was cast out.

### The Vea Lode Covenant, 49–51

The Vea Lode Covenant entered the period as a power engaged in a war of conquest, emerging from it with its seat secured by election but its territorial gains and internal stability shattered. The conflict with the Sworn Men of Meigate defined the early years, resulting in decisive victories for the Covenant and the total destruction of its enemy. However, the assassination of the ruler Keithfal Naell in year 50 triggered a collapse in the Covenant’s standing, allowing a rebellion at Threi Cut to rise against it by the end of the period.

In year 49, the Covenant declared war on the Sworn Men of Meigate over a long-standing grievance. This campaign yielded immediate results: the Covenant defeated the Sworn Men at Meigate in year 49, killing 149 men, and again in year 50, killing 124. Following the second victory, the Covenant took Meigate from the Sworn Men. The Sworn Men of Meigate was finished that same year, becoming landless as its last 12 followers scattered. The Covenant and the Sworn Men made peace in year 51, two years after the collapse of the Sworn Men.

The internal stability of the Covenant fractured in year 50 when Keithfal Naell, the ruler, was killed by Tor Nathgoull at Vea Lode. Herpeim Raern took the seat of the Vea Lode Covenant by election in year 50 following this death. Despite the new ruler, the Covenant’s standing had fallen to nothing by year 51. In that year, Threi Cut rose against the Covenant. The Hadale Commune also attempted a raid on Meigate in year 51, but it was beaten off.


## Reigns

_In the order they begin._

### The rule of Heth Fal over the Sworn Men of Laehiford, 39–51

Heth Fal took the seat of the Sworn Men of Laehiford in 39 after an election set aside the claim of the named heir Herpeim Raern. Herpeim was cast out, and Heth held the seat until the period ended in 51. The previous ruler, Math Ham, had died in 39. Heth’s rule began with the appointment of Teillmol Lund as steward in 40. The Sworn Men publicly slighted the Vea Lode Covenant in 41 and demanded tribute from them in 42, a demand that was refused. In 43, Heth ordered the murder of Thosruld Lul at Hadale. That same year, the Sworn Men’s raid on Vea Lode was beaten off.

The power’s cohesion eroded as five people were courted away from its ruler. Voudreirn Wer won Baedros Mam away in 48, then won Stonand Ker away in 49. Teillmol Lund won Baedros Mam away in 49. Voudreirn Wer won Draes Wild away in 50, and Thurnean Kourn won Draes Wild away in 51. The Sworn Men sent out three raids, all of which were beaten off: against Vea Lode in 43, Threi Cut in 44, and Griwick in 47. Seven exiles returned to take service during the period. One marriage tied the Sworn Men to the Kebarrow Compact in 44.

Teillmol Lund died in 50. The period ran for 13 years, from 39 to 51. Two people held the seat; one died and one remained holding. The Sworn Men of Laehiford finished the period with Heth Fal still holding the seat.

### The rule of Wuldweald Valdrith over the Kebarrow Compact, 51–51

Wuldweald Valdrith came to power in 51 through an election that set aside the claim of the named heir, Hehum Skul. Valdrith had contested Skul’s claim, and the electoral decision favored him. Skul was cast out of the Kebarrow Compact as the losing claimant. Valdrith took the seat of the Kebarrow Compact, ending Skul’s tenure.

During this same year, Valdrith ordered the murder of Paernmel Has at Kebarrow. Has had been the ruler of the Kebarrow Compact prior to his death. The killing occurred at Kebarrow, the settlement that served as the center of the Compact.

The period ended in 51 with Wuldweald Valdrith still holding the seat. No further changes in leadership occurred within these years.
