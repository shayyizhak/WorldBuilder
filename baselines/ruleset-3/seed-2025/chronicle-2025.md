# A history of the Foldkal Vale

Seed 2025, years 1–51. Every name, date and figure below is drawn from the simulation itself.


## Wars

_In the order they begin._

### The Thea Seam War: the Waeslefell Compact against the Baesveireach Compact, 3–7

The war began in year 3 when the Waeslefell Compact declared war on the Baesveireach Compact over Thea Seam. In that same year, the Waeslefell Compact defeated the Baesveireach Compact at Thea Seam, with 25 dead. The Baesveireach Compact reversed this outcome in year 4, defeating the Waeslefell Compact at Thea Seam with 21 dead. The Waeslefell Compact regained the advantage in year 5, defeating the Baesveireach Compact at Thea Seam with 17 dead and taking Thea Seam from the Baesveireach Compact.

In year 6, the Waeslefell Compact defeated the Baesveireach Compact at Baesveireach, with 89 dead, and took Baesveireach from the Baesveireach Compact. The conflict ended in year 7 when the two powers made peace after 4 years, following the collapse of the Baesveireach Compact.

### The War for Skomere: the Waeslefell Compact against the Deafil League, 21–24

The War for Skomere began in 21 when the Waeslefell Compact declared war on the Deafil League over a long-standing grievance. The conflict was fought at Skomere and Kremoor. In 21, the Waeslefell Compact defeated the Deafil League at Skomere, where 130 people died, and took the settlement from the League. Later that same year, the Compact defeated the League again at Kremoor, with 132 dead. In 23, the Waeslefell Compact defeated the Deafil League at Kremoor once more, resulting in 120 deaths.

The war ended in 24 when the Waeslefell Compact and the Deafil League made peace after three years of fighting, both sides exhausted. The period concluded in 26 when Ground Raeskur had Kae Voth murdered at Skomere. Attempts on Kae Voth’s life by Vaell Foull in 22 and Fur Roundpoull in 23 had failed and were traced back, but these events did not alter the course of the war itself.


## Powers

_In the order they begin._

### The Deafil League, 2–33

The Deafil League’s thirty-two years were defined by a cycle of failed aggression and internal collapse. At the start, the League bound itself to the Baesveireach Compact through the marriages of Featrein Roumi and Bage Busba, and later to the Waeslefell Compact through Steapall Faedild. These alliances did not secure peace. The League launched nine raids against the Waeslefell Compact between years 9 and 31, all of which were beaten off. In response, the Waeslefell Compact raided the League’s settlements three times, carrying off grain and killing residents. The League’s own strength eroded under famine, which killed 331 people in Skomere and Kremoor between years 13 and 16, while 602 others fled.

Internal instability accelerated the decline. Feasleith Husbill died in year 11, and Steapall Faedild took the seat by election, setting aside the named heir Bage Busba. Faedild’s rule ended in year 21 when he was cast out for an attempted murder, and the Waeslefell Compact defeated the League at Skomere, taking the settlement. Kae Voth took the seat by election but was killed in year 26. Touthseand Gourses succeeded him, only to be challenged and killed by Sealdvern Geitoun in year 27. Sealdvern was himself murdered by Vaepe Lirnkour in year 28. Deththou Keis took the seat by election, setting aside Vaepe Lirnkour, but was cast out in year 29 for attempted murder. Fouldraern Thraernveall took the seat by election.

The final years saw the League’s destruction. Fur Roundpoull challenged and cast out Fouldraern Thraernveall in year 32, but was himself cast out later that year for attempted murder. The Waeslefell Compact declared war and defeated the League at Kremoor, taking the settlement. The Deafil League was finished, its land lost and its last three followers scattering. The period ended with the League destroyed, having fought four battles, all lost, and held the seat for an average of 3.7 years across eight rulers.

### The Baesveireach Compact, 3–7

The Baesveireach Compact’s five-year existence ended in ruin, defined by a single, escalating quarrel with the Waeslefell Compact. The period began with the Waeslefell Compact declaring war over Thea Seam, a dispute that would consume four years and result in the total destruction of the Baesveireach Compact. Sildbeld Bouthwoun held the seat at the start, but his house ended during the conflict, leaving the power leaderless and landless.

The war opened in year 3 with a defeat for the Baesveireach Compact at Thea Seam, where 25 died. In year 4, Sildbeld Bouthwoun ordered the murder of Keirn Heapound at Waeslefell, and the Baesveireach Compact won a battle at Thea Seam against the Waeslefell Compact, with 21 dead. However, the tide turned in year 5. The Baesveireach Compact lost a battle at Thea Seam, with 17 dead, and the Waeslefell Compact took Thea Seam from them. Hunger at Thea Seam that year killed 3 people. Toulgaern Nein returned from exile and took service with the Baesveireach Compact, but the power continued to lose ground.

In year 6, the Baesveireach Compact’s raid on Waeslefell was beaten off. The Waeslefell Compact defeated the Baesveireach Compact at Baesveireach, with 89 dead, and took Baesveireach from them. The Baesveireach Compact was finished, its last 7 followers scattering. The period ended in year 7 with peace between the Waeslefell Compact and the Baesveireach Compact, after the collapse of the latter. The Baesveireach Compact had fought four battles, winning one and losing three, and lost two places.

### The Waeslefell Compact, 42–51

The Waeslefell Compact endured ten years of violent succession, marked by open challenges, conspiracies, and a plague that devastated its territory. The period began with Fraen Woudem holding the seat, a position he retained only to be displaced by Maldgeil Threassend in year 43. Maldgeil’s rule was brief and ended in murder at Waeslefell in year 44, after which Houwearn Mou took the seat by right of birth. Houwearn’s tenure was interrupted by a coup in year 49, when Ronith Wasnur seized the seat, leaving Houwearn cast out. Ronith ruled until his death in year 51, when Thraebaern Ramdem succeeded him by right of birth, still holding the seat as the period ended.

Internal instability defined the era. Four people were cast out: Fraen Woudem and Greandsam Kreir for losing open challenges, Woum Teldlei for conspiracy, and Houwearn Mou for losing a coup. Greandsam Kreir was also declared outlaw after his conspiracy was uncovered. One person, Maldgeil Threassend, was killed by Woum Teldlei. The seat passed from one person to another four times inside this period. Three people were courted away from its ruler: Ronith Wasnur won Haeth Tha away in 48 and Songoul Meandwur away in 49, while Dirnul Kar won Lel Neind away in 50.

External calamity struck Skomere in year 46, where a plague broke out and took 67 lives. In year 47, the sickness continued, killing 84 more and causing 150 to flee. The Waeslefell Compact ordered no killings against people of other powers, and its own people murdered from within, by its own people, numbered one. The period ran from 42 to 51 inclusive, which is 10 years. Five people held the seat, averaging 1.8 years each, with a median of 1.0 years. Two years were marked by hunger or sickness, killing 151 in all.


## Reigns

_In the order they begin._

### The rule of Thraebaern Ramdem over the Waeslefell Compact, 51–51

Thraebaern Ramdem assumed the seat of the Waeslefell Compact in 51, succeeding by right of birth following the death of Ronith Wasnur. Wasnur, who had ruled the Waeslefell Compact, died in Waeslefell in 51 at the age of twenty-four. Thraebaern’s accession marked the beginning of a reign that lasted only for the remainder of that year.

The period was defined by the deaths of two key figures. Ronith Wasnur’s death in Waeslefell enabled Thraebaern’s succession. Shortly thereafter, Dirnul Kar, a retainer of the Waeslefell Compact, died in Skomere in 51 at the age of eighteen. These events concluded the era, with Thraebaern holding the seat as the year ended.
