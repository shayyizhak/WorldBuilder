# A history of the Tholdlael Vale

Seed 99, years 1–51. Every name, date and figure below is drawn from the simulation itself.


## Wars

_In the order they begin._

### The the Peirnhewick Crown Aggression: the Peirnhewick Crown against the Staernwefell Crown, 3–5

In year 3, the Peirnhewick Crown declared war on the Staernwefell Crown over Sa Cut. The Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Staernwefell Crown at Sa Cut, where 30 died, and took the site from them.

In year 4, the Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Staernwefell Crown at Staernwefell, where 132 died.

In year 5, the Peirnhewick Crown and the Staernwefell Crown made peace after 2 years, both sides exhausted.

### The the Peirnhewick Crown Aggression of 20: the Peirnhewick Crown against the Pistead Compact, 20–21

In 20, the Peirnhewick Crown declared war on the Pistead Compact, citing a long-standing grievance. The conflict began with a decisive engagement at Pistead, where the Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Pistead Compact, resulting in 240 deaths. This victory allowed the Peirnhewick Crown to take the settlement of Pistead from the Pistead Compact later that same year.

The war lasted for one year. In 21, the Peirnhewick Crown and the Pistead Compact made peace following the collapse of the Pistead Compact. The period ended with the Peirnhewick Crown holding Pistead and the Pistead Compact no longer existing as a power.


## Powers

_In the order they begin._

### The Peirnhewick Crown, 2–21

The period from 2 to 21 saw the Peirnhewick Crown shift through five rulers, its authority defined by violent succession and territorial expansion. The era began with Hounu Laweim binding the Crown to the Pistead Compact through marriage, but his rule ended in year 5 when he was cast out after an attempt on Bi Froullgeis failed. Bi Froullgeis was subsequently murdered by Dar Taldwond, who took the seat by right of birth. Dar Taldwond died in year 6, and Thothbus Treim succeeded him, only to be killed by Hounu Laweim in a coup in year 7. Hounu Laweim’s standing fell very low as Sa Cut and Nae Seam rose against him, yet he maintained power until his own death in year 17. Wimnall Nind then took the seat by right of birth and held it through the end of the period.

Military action characterized the Crown’s external relations. It fought three wars, winning all three battles: defeating the Staernwefell Crown at Sa Cut in year 3, at Staernwefell in year 4, and the Pistead Compact at Pistead in year 20. These victories allowed the Crown to take Sa Cut from the Staernwefell Crown and Pistead from the Pistead Compact. The Crown sent three raids, all carrying off plunder from Pistead in years 8 and 17 and from Kraefell in year 9. It suffered five raids, including one from the Staernwefell Crown in year 21 that was beaten off. The quarrel between the Peirnhewick Crown and the Staernwefell Crown persisted through two battles, while the Pistead Compact was finished in year 20 after losing Pistead.

Internal stability was undermined by famine and plague. Nine years of hunger or sickness killed 586 people and caused 322 to flee their homes. Nae Seam suffered repeated famines starting in year 4, and Peirnhewick was struck by plague in years 12 and 13. The Crown ordered one killing against a person of another power, Dinthei Lemmar at Pistead in year 18, and experienced two internal murders. Two marriages tied the Crown to the Pistead Compact in years 2 and 3, to the Staernwefell Crown in year 4, and to the Sworn Men of Kraefell in year 21. Two places, Sa Cut and Nae Seam, rose against the Crown when its standing had fallen very low.

### The Sworn Men of Kraefell, 19–31

The Sworn Men of Kraefell began their independence in year 19 by breaking from the Pistead Compact, with Draeldreil Thraellvu taking the seat. The period was defined by internal instability and external pressure, culminating in the power’s destruction by the Peirnhewick Crown. Draeldreil Thraellvu held the seat until his death in year 30, but his rule was marked by violence and the loss of territory.

Ba Turtheill returned from exile in year 21 to serve the Sworn Men, but was killed by Thrallheirn Gend at Kraefell in year 24. Thethden Steith also returned from exile in year 29 to take service with the Sworn Men. In that same year, Draeldreil Thraellvu had Vour Trouthteith murdered at Staernwefell. The Peirnhewick Crown declared war on the Sworn Men over a long-standing grievance, defeating them at Kraefell with 96 dead.

The Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Sworn Men again at Kraefell in year 30, killing 215 and taking the settlement. Draeldreil Thraellvu died at Kraefell in year 30. The Sworn Men of Kraefell was finished, becoming landless with its last follower scattering. The Peirnhewick Crown and the Sworn Men of Kraefell made peace in year 31 after two years of conflict following the collapse of the Sworn Men.

### Free Puholt, 36–49

Free Puholt emerged from the Sworn Men of Pistead in 36, with Hedroum Traern taking the seat. The period was defined by internal instability and external failure. Four people held the seat in fourteen years, three of them cast out. The power sent out three raids, all beaten off.

Hedroum Traern’s rule ended in 42 after an attempt on Gaedrell Meand failed and was traced back. He was cast out for attempted murder. Goth Beildbea took the seat by election. His rule also ended in 43, after an attempt on Thethden Steith failed and was traced back. He was cast out for attempted murder.

Ros Vur took the seat in 43, raised from Puholt with no claimant remaining. Free Puholt’s raid on Sa Cut was beaten off in 44, and its raid on Nae Seam was beaten off in 45. Ros Vur’s rule ended in 49 after an attempt on Beind Grorn failed and was traced back. He was cast out for attempted murder. Vi Thrinli took the seat by election in 49, still holding at the end of this period.

## Reigns

_In the order they begin._

### The rule of Beind Grorn over the Peirnhewick Crown, 48–51

Beind Grorn took the seat of the Peirnhewick Crown in 48 after setting aside the claim of the named heir Fum Seas, who had inherited the power by primogeniture. The succession followed the death of the previous ruler, Tharnpeil Waeld, who was killed by Vold Freideth earlier that year. Beind’s rule began with the division of the Peirnhewick Crown, as Fum Seas carried off two places to form Free Kraefell. In the same year, Beind’s child Porn Hil was born at Peirnhewick.

The new ruler faced immediate external threats and internal conspiracies. Free Kraefell declared war over Nae Seam, while the Peirnhewick Crown continued its war against the Sworn Men of Pistead, defeating them at Pistead with 271 dead. Beind had Vold Freideth murdered at Staernwefell in 49. That year, two conspiracies against Beind were uncovered: Trapearn Sael was cast out after four years of plotting, and Nothmeand Trearol was put to death after one year. The Peirnhewick Crown defeated Free Kraefell at Nae Seam with 22 dead. In 50, Beind had Doul Wein murdered at Staernwefell, and the Peirnhewick Crown defeated Free Kraefell again at Nae Seam with 18 dead. Peace was made with the Sworn Men of Pistead after two years of war.

In 51, the Peirnhewick Crown defeated Free Kraefell at Nae Seam with 16 dead. Brou Kurn won Fuld Deand away from the ruler, and Ve Va was raised to steward. The Peirnhewick Crown publicly slighted Free Puholt. The period ended in 51 with Beind Grorn still holding the seat.

### The rule of Veall Lea over the Sworn Men of Pistead, 50–51

In year 50, the seat of the Sworn Men of Pistead became vacant when Doul Wein, the previous ruler, was killed by Beind Grorn at Staernwefell. The Peirnhewick Crown and the Sworn Men of Pistead made peace after two years of conflict, leaving both sides exhausted. Ger Draelgall married Brou Kurn, binding the Sworn Men of Pistead to the Peirnhewick Crown. Steisear Hearn returned from exile and took service with the Sworn Men of Pistead.

Veall Lea contested the claim of Steisear Hearn, the named heir, to the Sworn Men of Pistead. The contest was decided by primogeniture, but Veall Lea took the seat, setting aside the claim of the named heir Steisear Hearn. Steisear Hearn was cast out of the Sworn Men of Pistead.

In year 51, the Sworn Men of Pistead spent 95 silver to buy back goodwill. The period ended in 51 with Veall Lea still holding the seat.
