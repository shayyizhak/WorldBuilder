# A history of the Rethkeis Vale

Seed 42, years 1–51. Every name, date and figure below is drawn from the simulation itself.


## Wars

_In the order they begin._

### The War for Threi Cut: the Griwick Compact against the Wurn League, 5–8

The Griwick Compact declared war on the Wurn League in year 5 over Threi Cut. The Wurn League defeated the Griwick Compact at Threi Cut in year 5, with 45 dead. The Griwick Compact defeated the Wurn League at Threi Cut in year 6, with 35 dead.

In year 7, the Griwick Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Threi Cut, with 29 dead. The Griwick Compact took Threi Cut from the Wurn League in year 7. The Griwick Compact and the Wurn League made peace in year 8 after 3 years, both sides exhausted.

### The War for Threi Cut of 7: the Kebarrow Compact against the Wurn League, 7–10

The Kebarrow Compact declared war on the Wurn League in year 7 for Threi Cut. In that same year, the Kebarrow Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Laehiford, where 139 died, and took the settlement from the Wurn League.

In year 8, the Kebarrow Compact and its allies defeated the Wurn League at Hadale, where 125 died. In year 9, they defeated the Wurn League again at Hadale, where 109 died, and took the settlement from the Wurn League.

In year 10, the Kebarrow Compact and the Wurn League made peace after 3 years, after the collapse of the Wurn League.


## Powers

_In the order they begin._

### The Kebarrow Compact, 2–21

The period from 2 to 21 was defined by the Kebarrow Compact’s rapid ascent and subsequent collapse. It began bound to the Griwick Compact through the marriages of Searn Sisrill and Deargund Keirem, and ended with Math Ham holding the seat while the power had lost three places and its standing had fallen to nothing. The Compact fought six battles, winning three against the Wurn League and losing three to the Griwick Compact. It sent out seven raids and suffered seven, while six people were cast out and two exiles returned.

In the early years, the Compact leveraged its alliance with the Griwick Compact to defeat the Wurn League. After declaring war in 7, it defeated the Wurn League at Laehiford in 7 and at Hadale in 8 and 9, taking both places. Searn Sisrill was killed in 8, and Reweld Wul took the seat by election. Reweld Wul’s rule was marked by internal dissent and external conflict; he was cast out in 17 after an attempt on Pouldrir Ho failed. During this time, the Griwick Compact defeated the Compact at Meigate in 16 and at Kebarrow in 17, taking Meigate.

Trem Lolkoll took the seat in 17 but was killed by Nael War in 18. Math Ham then contested the claim of Sou Dra and took the seat, casting out Sou Dra. Laehiford rose against the Compact in 18 as its standing fell to nothing, breaking away as the Sworn Men of Laehiford in 19. Hadale followed in 20. Math Ham faced multiple conspiracies and attempts on his life, casting out Nael War, Thra Bround, and Weallhous Dreld. The period ended with Math Ham still holding the seat, but the Compact had lost Laehiford and Hadale and was exhausted from its wars with the Griwick Compact.

### The Griwick Compact, 4–23

The Griwick Compact’s twenty-year span from 4 to 23 was defined by a cycle of external aggression and internal collapse. It began as a raiding power, striking Kebarrow in year 4 to relieve famine at Vea Lode, and ended with its standing fallen to nothing after a coup in year 23. The period saw five rulers, with the seat passing four times. Three were killed, one cast out, and one held the seat at the end. The Compact fought six battles, losing only once to the Wurn League at Threi Cut in year 5, but winning five others against the Wurn League and the Kebarrow Compact. It sent six raids, all successful, and suffered five raids, two of which were beaten off.

Pouldrir Ho ruled from the start until year 19. His tenure included the war with the Wurn League over Threi Cut, which ended in peace in year 8 after three years of fighting. The Compact took Threi Cut from the Wurn League in year 7. Internal stability eroded as exiles returned and conspiracies emerged. Thulgea Bu returned in year 10 but was cast out in year 15 for conspiracy. Kei Leassel was also cast out in year 16 for conspiracy. In year 16, Vea Lode rose against the Compact, whose standing had fallen to nothing. The Compact then fought the Kebarrow Compact, taking Meigate in year 16 and winning battles at Meigate, Kebarrow, and Laehiford in years 16, 17, and 18. Peace with Kebarrow followed in year 19.

Pouldrir Ho was killed by Math Ham in year 19. Reweld Wul contested the heir Befu Seirn’s claim and took the seat in year 19, casting out Befu Seirn. Reweld Wul was murdered by Turaer Danpa in year 20. Heillvar Maer took the seat in year 20 by the strongest claim. Heillvar Maer married Theald Va in year 22, binding the Compact to the Sworn Men of Hadale. Turaer Danpa challenged Heillvar Maer in year 22, winning and taking the seat by coup, killing Heillvar Maer. Bu Rumpirn was cast out in year 22 for conspiracy. Thold Valmaer challenged Turaer Danpa in year 23, winning and taking the seat by coup, casting out Turaer Danpa. Threi Cut rose against the Compact in year 23 as its standing fell to nothing. Thold Valmaer held the seat at the end of the period.

### The Sworn Men of Laehiford, 19–35

The Sworn Men of Laehiford spent seventeen years as an independent power, beginning with a break from the Kebarrow Compact and ending in total destruction by the Griwick Compact. The period was defined by a brief consolidation of authority under Sou Dra, followed by a series of failed external raids and internal purges that weakened the faction before its final defeat.

Bour Gel took the seat in 19 when Laehiford broke from the Kebarrow Compact. He held the seat until 22, when Sou Dra challenged him openly, won, and took the seat. Bour Gel was cast out as the losing side of that open challenge. Sou Dra bound the Sworn Men of Laehiford to the Sworn Men of Hadale in 24 by marrying Heth Fal. He also ordered the murder of Thold Valmaer at Griwick in 28 and Thurnean Kourn at Griwick in 29.

The faction’s standing deteriorated through failed aggression and internal loss. Four raids sent out between 26 and 33 were all beaten off. Sou Dra had Turaer Danpa murdered at Laehiford in 32. A plague broke out at Laehiford in 33, killing 56 people. The Griwick Compact declared war in 34 over a long-standing grievance. The Sworn Men of Laehiford were defeated at Laehiford in 34, with 107 dead. The Griwick Compact took Laehiford from them in 34. The Sworn Men of Laehiford was finished, landless, with its last 8 followers scattering.

### The Sworn Men of Hadale, 20–39

The Sworn Men of Hadale spent twenty years in a state of internal fragmentation and external decline. Beginning with Sothkel Sald taking the seat in 20 after Hadale broke from the Kebarrow Compact, the power relied on a succession by election that failed to secure stability. Five people held the seat in this period, each ruling for an average of 3.6 years, with the seat passing between them four times. The group’s cohesion eroded through repeated purges and coups, while its military efforts against the Griwick Compact consistently failed.

Sothkel Sald’s rule ended in 27 when he was cast out after an attempt on Thold Valmaer failed. Beas Krouthea took the seat by election in 27, but his tenure was marked by conspiracy and violence. Tor Nathgoull challenged and defeated Beas Krouthea in 29, taking the seat by coup. Tor Nathgoull ordered the murder of Leimmil Theall at Griwick in 30, but was himself murdered by Stonand Ker at Hadale in 31. Stonand Ker then took the seat by coup in 32 after defeating Bour Gel. His rule ended in 38 when he was cast out following a failed attempt on Sou Dra.

The power’s final years were defined by war with the Griwick Compact. The Griwick Compact raided Hadale in 36, carrying off 44 ore and killing 12. In 38, the Griwick Compact declared war over a long-standing grievance and defeated the Sworn Men of Hadale at Hadale, with 79 dead. The Griwick Compact took Hadale from the Sworn Men of Hadale in 38, and the Sworn Men of Hadale was finished, its last 28 followers scattering. The Griwick Compact and the Sworn Men of Hadale made peace in 39. Throughout the period, the Sworn Men of Hadale sent out two raids, both beaten off, and suffered one raid. Five people were cast out, and three of its own people were murdered from within.

### The Kebarrow Compact, 22–41

The Kebarrow Compact’s twenty-year span from 22 to 41 was defined by internal instability and external defeat. The period began with Math Ham holding the seat, a position he retained after defeating Witrou Breim’s open challenge in 22. The Compact’s military posture was uniformly unsuccessful; all three raids it sent out against Vea Lode, Meigate, and Threi Cut between 24 and 29 were beaten off. Its only raid suffered, on Kebarrow in 26 by the Griwick Compact, also failed to take anything. The turning point came in 30 when the Griwick Compact declared war, defeated the Kebarrow Compact at Kebarrow, and took the settlement, ending the Compact’s control of its namesake place.

Succession within the Compact was volatile, with the seat passing seven times among eight rulers. Math Ham was cast out in 27 after losing an open challenge to Teillmol Lund, who was killed later that year. Wilwound Ska took the seat by setting aside the claim of the named heir Le Vild, but was himself killed by Gatros Hearn after losing an open challenge in 28. Gatros Hearn was cast out in 31 for attempted murder, allowing Paernrom Sir to take the seat by setting aside the claim of the named heir Hollsan Troull. Paernrom Sir was murdered in 32, and Kou Peis took the seat by election. Kou Peis was cast out in 34 after losing a coup to Kondruth Tru, who was murdered in 37, leading to Durnrin Drar taking the seat by election. Durnrin Drar remained in power at the end of the period.

The Compact’s cohesion eroded through purges and shifting alliances. Three of its own people were murdered from within, and nine were cast out for various offenses including conspiracy and failed challenges. One person already gone was declared outlaw. The Compact bound itself to other powers through four marriages: to the Sworn Men of Hadale in 25, and to the Griwick Compact in 32, 39, and 40. Despite these ties, fourteen people were courted away from its rulers, signaling a house coming apart. The period ended with Durnrin Drar still holding the seat, but the Compact had lost Kebarrow and suffered a decisive military defeat.

### The Sworn Men of Meigate, 26–51

The Sworn Men of Meigate spent twenty-six years as a fractured power, defined by a persistent inability to project force beyond its borders and a succession of internal purges that eroded its leadership. From its break from the Griwick Compact in 26, the group maintained a defensive posture, sending seven raids that were uniformly beaten off. Its internal stability was equally fragile, marked by the return of exiles who often turned against the seat, culminating in a devastating war that left the settlement exhausted but intact.

Weavel Fel held the seat from 26 until 39, a period characterized by the return of seven exiles who took service with the group, including Sothkel Sald, Math Ham, and Gatros Hearn. Despite this influx of personnel, Weavel’s rule ended when Gatros Hearn challenged him openly in 39, winning the seat and casting Weavel out. Gatros Hearn’s tenure was brief; he was killed by Sou Dra at Meigate in 41. Stonand Ker took the seat by election in 41, but his rule was immediately threatened by Sothkel Sald, whose failed attempt on Stonand’s life in 42 led to Sothkel’s exile and subsequent outlawry.

The period’s defining conflict was the war with the Griwick Compact, declared in 43 over long-standing grievances. The Sworn Men of Meigate lost both battles fought during this war, at Meigate in 43 with 192 dead and at Meigate in 44 with 160 dead. The fighting lasted two years, ending in peace in 45 with both sides exhausted. The settlement suffered a famine in 38 that killed 49 people, and the Griwick Compact raided Meigate in 40, killing 29 but taking nothing. By 51, Vuld Meisreill returned from exile to take service, while Stonand Ker remained holding the seat, having survived the war and the internal purges that had claimed his predecessors.

### The Kebarrow Compact, 42–44

The Kebarrow Compact’s rule in these years was defined by its integration with the Griwick Compact, a shift marked by two strategic marriages rather than internal consolidation. At the start of the period, the Compact held its seat through Durnrin Drar, who maintained power by aligning his household with the neighboring power. This alignment was formalized in year 43 when Durnrin Drar married Paendoull Maedrael, binding the Kebarrow Compact to the Griwick Compact. The union served as the primary structural feature of the era, linking the two entities through kinship rather than conquest or treaty.

The stability of this arrangement was challenged from within by Mald Krend, whose actions against the seat were uncovered in year 44 after three years of concealment. The conspiracy was not merely a political disagreement but a direct attempt to seize authority, leading to Mald Krend’s removal from the Kebarrow Compact. His casting out ended his influence and demonstrated that the seat remained secure against internal subversion, even as external ties deepened.

In the same year, the bond with the Griwick Compact was further solidified by a second marriage. Draes Wild married Theaki Houl, reinforcing the connection established by Durnrin Drar’s earlier union. By the end of the period, the Kebarrow Compact had not expanded its territory or altered its succession method, but it had become inextricably linked to the Griwick Compact through two generations of marriage, while its internal leadership had expelled a conspirator to maintain its hold on the seat.

### The Griwick Compact, 44–51

The Griwick Compact’s eight-year span from 44 to 51 was defined by violent internal collapse and the rapid turnover of its leadership. At the start, the power was bound to the Kebarrow Compact through the marriage of Fouthgo Souldsound and Keithfal Naell, and it held the upper hand in external conflict, defeating the Sworn Men of Meigate at Meigate in 44 with 160 dead. However, the period ended with the seat held by Sothkel Sald, who had seized it by coup in 50, while the Compact’s standing had fallen to nothing following a rebellion by Threi Cut in 47.

The seat passed from one person to another four times inside this period. Sou Dra, who had held the seat since 34, was killed by Heth Fal in 47 after Heth Fal challenged and won against him. Heth Fal’s rule was brief; he was killed by Fouthgo Souldsound in 47 after Fouthgo challenged and won against him. Fouthgo then took the seat by coup, but his rule ended when Paernmel Has challenged him openly in 48, won, and took the seat. Paernmel was killed by Sothkel Sald in 50 after Sothkel challenged and won against him. Three of the five people who held the seat were killed, and one was cast out.

Internal violence and purges eroded the Compact’s cohesion. Fouthgo Souldsound was cast out in 47 for an attempted murder on Sou Dra that failed and was traced back. Sothkel Sald was cast out in 49 for an attempted murder on Paernmel Has that failed and was traced back. Vuld Meisreill was cast out in 50 for an attempted murder on Paernmel Has that failed and was traced back. Vuld Meisreill was later declared outlaw in 51 for a conspiracy against Sothkel Sald that was uncovered after two years. One exile, Mald Krend, returned in 46 and took service with the Compact. The Compact suffered one raid on Griwick in 45 by the Sworn Men of Meigate, which was beaten off. Seven people were courted away from its ruler during this time.
