### The Staernwefell Crown, 4–9

The period from 4 to 9 saw the Staernwefell Crown collapse from a seated power into a landless remnant. It began with Stealmaell Nothmi holding the seat, but his death in 4 triggered a succession by strongest claim, which Faeth Paes took. The stability of the crown was fragile; within five years, the seat had passed four times. Faespa Futhbill won a challenge against Faeth Paes in 5 and took the seat by coup, casting Faeth Paes out. Vopeis Foldthous then had Faespa Futhbill murdered in 7, allowing Dinthei Lemmar to take the seat by strongest claim. Vopeis Foldthous challenged and defeated Dinthei Lemmar in 8, taking the seat by coup and casting Dinthei out.

The crown’s external position deteriorated rapidly. The Peirnhewick Crown declared war on the Staernwefell Crown in 7 for Sa Cut, breaking its alliance with them. The Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Staernwefell Crown at Sa Cut in 7, where 29 died, and took the site. In 8, the Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Staernwefell Crown at Staernwefell, where 115 died, and took the settlement. The Staernwefell Crown was finished in 8, its last 6 followers scattering. A raid on Puholt in 8 was beaten off. The period ended in 9 with peace between the Peirnhewick Crown and the Staernwefell Crown, after the collapse of the Staernwefell Crown.

Internal decay accompanied the external losses. The crown suffered one year of hunger at Sa Cut in 5, killing 7. Two people were courted away from its ruler: Vopeis Foldthous was won away by Faespa Futhbill in 5, and Dinthei Lemmar was won away by Vopeis Foldthous in 6. Two people were cast out, both as the losing side of a coup. One person was killed by their own people. The quarrel between the Peirnhewick Crown and the Staernwefell Crown resulted in two battles, both lost by the Staernwefell Crown.

### The Pistead Compact, 6–22

The Pistead Compact’s seventeen-year existence was defined by violent succession and a fatal rivalry with the Peirnhewick Crown. It began in year 6 with a plague at Pistead that killed 363 people. The power’s internal stability was fragile, with six people holding the seat in total. Hounu Laweim returned from exile in year 7 to serve the Compact, but Beald Feithsith had Vasdo Dri murdered at Pistead that same year. Krullkell Skild took the seat by election, only to be challenged and defeated by Beald Feithsith, who seized the seat by coup and cast out Krullkell.

Beald Feithsith’s rule was marked by retaliation against the Peirnhewick Crown. After the Peirnhewick Crown raided Kraefell in year 9, the Compact raided Peirnhewick, carrying off 55 grain and killing 24. The Peirnhewick Crown declared war in year 11 over a long-standing grievance. The Compact defeated the Peirnhewick Crown at Pistead, killing 64, and again at Kraefell, killing 54. Peace was made in year 13 after two years of war. The Compact continued to raid, taking Puholt and Staernwefell, but its raids on Sa Cut and Nae Seam were beaten off. Dinthei Lemmar returned from exile in year 10 and later challenged Ba Turtheill openly in year 15, winning the seat and casting out Ba Turtheill.

The Compact’s decline accelerated after Dinthei Lemmar was killed by Krullkell Skild in year 18. Goth Beildbea took the seat by election and married Neldthear Throum, binding the Compact to the Second Crown of Kraefell. Thethden Steith married Vour Trouthteith, binding the Compact to the Burghers of Staernwefell. However, the Peirnhewick Crown declared war again in year 21, broke its alliance, and defeated the Compact at Pistead, killing 185. The Peirnhewick Crown took Pistead, and the Compact was finished, its last 10 followers scattering. Peace was made in year 22 after the collapse.

### The Burghers of Staernwefell, 13–39

The Burghers of Staernwefell began their twenty-seven-year existence as a breakaway from the Peirnhewick Crown, establishing independence under Duth Traellkeild. The period was defined by a long-standing grievance with Peirnhewick that escalated into open warfare, and by a pattern of failed aggression against neighboring powers. Duth Traellkeild held the seat for two decades, maintaining power through election and force, until his rule ended in 33 when he was cast out for attempted murder.

Duth Traellkeild’s tenure was marked by a series of nine raids, eight of which were beaten off by the Peirnhewick Crown, the Second Crown of Kraefell, or local defenses. Raids on Kraefell in 14, Peirnhewick in 16, Puholt in 21, Pistead in 22, Sa Cut in 23, Nae Seam in 24, Peirnhewick in 32, and Puholt in 37 all failed. Only the raid on Kraefell in 38 succeeded, carrying off 22 grain and killing 37. Internal stability was fragile; Ba Turtheill returned from exile in 16, and Goth Beildbea returned in 32. In 30, Vour Trouthteith challenged Duth openly and lost, leading to Vour’s killing by Duth at Staernwefell.

The conflict with the Peirnhewick Crown intensified in 37 when Peirnhewick declared war. Goth Beildbea took the seat by election in 33 after Duth’s exile. Under Goth, the Burghers suffered two defeats at Staernwefell in 37 and 38, with 128 and 107 dead respectively. In 37, Goth ordered the murder of Gaedrell Meand at Pistead. The Peirnhewick Crown took Staernwefell in 38, and the Burghers of Staernwefell were finished, its last 5 followers scattering. Peace was made with the Peirnhewick Crown in 39.

### The Second Crown of Kraefell, 15–44

The Second Crown of Kraefell existed for thirty years, from 15 to 44, as a breakaway power that began with independence and ended with total destruction. It started when Kraefell broke from the Pistead Compact in 15, with Neldthear Throum taking the seat. The period was defined by a long-standing grievance with the Peirnhewick Crown, which resulted in two battles lost by Kraefell and the eventual loss of its territory. The power also suffered internal instability, marked by a coup in 38 and a plague in 17 that killed 323 people.

Neldthear Throum held the seat from 15 until 38. His rule was marked by external pressure and internal dissent. In 17, a plague at Kraefell killed 323 people. The Peirnhewick Crown raided Kraefell in 25, carrying off 41 grain and killing 37. In 29, Kraefell swore an alliance with the Burghers of Staernwefell. Neldthear’s authority was challenged when Maeld Brei returned from exile in 32 and took service with the crown. Maeld Brei later won Thethden Steith away from Neldthear in 38. In 38, Maeld Brei challenged Neldthear Throum for the seat and won. Neldthear was cast out, and Maeld Brei took the seat by coup.

Maeld Brei’s rule was brief and ended in defeat. He faced repeated raids from the Peirnhewick Crown, which declared war on Kraefell in 42 over the long-standing grievance. The Peirnhewick Crown defeated Kraefell at Kraefell in 42, with 207 dead, and again in 43, with 195 dead. In 43, the Peirnhewick Crown took Kraefell from the Second Crown of Kraefell. The Second Crown of Kraefell was finished, landless, with its last 12 followers scattering. Peace was made between the Peirnhewick Crown and the Second Crown of Kraefell in 44, after the collapse of the Second Crown of Kraefell.

### Free Pistead, 37–47

Free Pistead began in 37 as a breakaway from the Peirnhewick Crown, with Geabum Stull carrying off two places to establish his own rule. For the first eight years of its existence, the new power projected strength through raids, though most were repelled. It sent five raids in total: one against Peirnhewick in 39, one against Staernwefell in 43, one against Sa Cut in 44, and one against Nae Seam in 45 were all beaten off. Only the raid on Kraefell in 40 succeeded, carrying off 37 grain and killing 36. The period saw one return from exile, Thes Brendweir, who took service with Free Pistead in 42.

The balance shifted in 45 when the Peirnhewick Crown declared war over a long-standing grievance. Free Pistead fought two battles against Peirnhewick, both lost. At Puholt in 45, 277 of Free Pistead’s forces died, and Peirnhewick took the place. In 46, the conflict moved to Pistead, where 305 died in defeat and Peirnhewick took the settlement. During this time, Geabum Stull was killed by Goth Beildbea at Pistead.

Free Pistead finished in 46, landless, with its last 6 followers scattering after the loss of Pistead. The Peirnhewick Crown and Free Pistead made peace in 47, ending two years of war after the collapse of the latter. The period ran for 11 years, during which one person held the seat, one war was begun, and two places were lost to Peirnhewick.

### The Sworn Men of Pistead, 47–50

The period from 47 to 50 saw the Sworn Men of Pistead transition from a branch of the Peirnhewick Crown to an independent line under new leadership. At the start, Neldthear Throum held the seat, having secured it by taking three places from the Peirnhewick Crown in 47. His rule ended with his death in Pistead in 50, at the age of 71.

Vi Thrinli, born in 23, returned from exile in 49 and entered the service of the Sworn Men of Pistead. Following Neldthear’s death, Vi Thrinli took the seat by right of birth in 50, completing the succession.

### The rule of Nothmeand Trearol over the Peirnhewick Crown, 51–51

In year 51, Duth Traellkeild challenged Maeld Brei openly for the Peirnhewick Crown and won the seat. Maeld Brei was killed by Duth Traellkeild at Peirnhewick. Trapearn Sael was raised to steward of the Peirnhewick Crown. Nothmeand Trearol challenged Duth Traellkeild for the Peirnhewick Crown and won.

Nothmeand Trearol took the seat of the Peirnhewick Crown by coup. Duth Traellkeild was killed by Nothmeand Trearol at Peirnhewick. Free Kraefell and the Peirnhewick Crown made peace after 2 years, both sides exhausted.
