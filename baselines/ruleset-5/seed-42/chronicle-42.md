The war began in 27 when the Kebarrow Compact declared war on the Griwick Compact over a long-standing grievance. The Griwick Compact defeated the Kebarrow Compact at Vea Lode in the same year, with 57 dead. The Griwick Compact defeated the Kebarrow Compact at Vea Lode again in 28, with 48 dead.

In 28, Sou Dra had Thold Valmaer murdered at Griwick. The conflict continued until 29, when the Kebarrow Compact and the Griwick Compact made peace after 2 years, both sides exhausted.

### The Wurn League, 2–21

The Wurn League’s twenty-year span from 2 to 21 began with territorial expansion and ended with territorial contraction, defined by a single, protracted conflict with the Griwick Compact. The period opened with the League occupying Threi Cut, a site previously unheld, and securing alliances through six marriages to the Kebarrow Compact and the Griwick Compact. These diplomatic ties, however, could not prevent the Griwick Compact from declaring war on the League in year 5 over Threi Cut. The resulting five-year war saw the League suffer four defeats and lose two places, Threi Cut and Laehiford, to the Griwick Compact.

Internal instability accompanied the external losses. Reweld Wul was cast out in year 7 for attempted murder, allowing Math Ham to take the seat by setting aside the claim of the named heir Witrou Breim. Math Ham’s rule was marked by violence, including the murder of Searn Sisrill at Kebarrow, but ended when he was killed by Kei Leassel in year 10. Trem Lolkoll then took the seat by right of birth and held it until the period’s end. The League’s military posture shifted from aggression to failure; of seven raids sent out, four were beaten off and two took nothing, while five raids against the League succeeded in carrying off plunder.

The conflict with the Griwick Compact consumed the era’s resources. The League fought five battles against the Griwick Compact, winning only one at Threi Cut in year 5 before losing four subsequent engagements. A famine at Threi Cut in year 6 killed 13 people and left grain short by 8. The war concluded in year 10 with both sides exhausted, but the League had lost Threi Cut in year 7 and Laehiford in year 8. The period closed with Trem Lolkoll still holding the seat, the League diminished in territory and prestige, and the Griwick Compact as the dominant regional power.

### The Wurn League, 22–35

The Wurn League’s fourteen-year span from 22 to 35 was defined by internal collapse and external defeat. The period began with Trem Lolkoll holding the seat, but his rule was immediately undermined by conspiracy. Befu Seirn’s plot against him was uncovered in 22, leading to Seirn’s execution. This instability marked the start of a rapid succession of rulers, with the seat passing eight times among nine people. The average tenure was 1.3 years, and the median was just one year. The League’s authority eroded through a series of coups, castings-out, and murders, leaving it vulnerable to the Griwick Compact.

Internal strife drove the early years. Nael War’s conspiracy against Trem Lolkoll was uncovered in 25, resulting in War’s casting out. In 26, Gatros Hearn challenged and defeated Trem Lolkoll, taking the seat by coup before being killed by Sothkel Sald later that same year. Sothkel Sald himself was killed by Saern Meastouth in 26, who then took the seat. Saern Meastouth’s rule ended in 27 when his attempt on Thold Valmaer failed, leading to his casting out. Teapeall Heampim took the seat by right of birth in 27 and died in 28. Paernrom Sir contested the claim of the named heir Bu Rumpirn in 28, winning by primogeniture and casting out Bu Rumpirn. Paernrom Sir was murdered by Stonand Ker in 30, and Heillvar Maer took the seat by right of birth. Heillvar Maer was cast out in 32 after an attempt on Nael War failed, and Drarka Draernthun took the seat by right of birth.

The League’s external position deteriorated alongside its internal chaos. It suffered one raid by the Griwick Compact in 24, which carried off 34 grain and killed 16. Its own three raids against the Griwick Compact in 29 and 31 were all beaten off. In 33, the Griwick Compact declared war and broke its alliance with the League. Stonand Ker challenged and defeated Drarka Draernthun in 33, taking the seat by coup and casting out Drarka. The Griwick Compact defeated the Wurn League at Hadale in 33, with 58 dead, and again in 34, with 87 dead. In 34, the Griwick Compact took Hadale from the Wurn League. The Wurn League was finished, landless, with its last 25 followers scattering. Peace was made between the Griwick Compact and the Wurn League in 35, after the League’s collapse.

### The Griwick Compact, 24–43

The Griwick Compact’s twenty-year span from 24 to 43 was defined by violent succession, territorial expansion, and the destruction of its rivals. The period began with a coup that installed Bu Rumpirn, who was immediately ousted by Thold Valmaer, establishing a pattern of instability where the seat passed seven times among eight rulers. Despite internal churn, the Compact fought six battles, winning all of them, and expanded its territory by taking Hadale from the Wurn League and Kebarrow from the Kebarrow Compact. The Wurn League and the Kebarrow Compact were both finished during this time, leaving the Griwick Compact as the dominant regional power, though it suffered three major plagues that killed 640 people and caused 510 to flee.

Thold Valmaer held the seat from 24 until his death in 28, when Sou Dra killed him. Nael War took the seat in 28 by the strongest claim and ruled until he was killed by Stonand Ker in 34. Kondruth Tru succeeded him in 34, also by the strongest claim, and was killed by Beas Krouthea in 37. Durnrin Drar took the seat in 37 and was killed by Stonand Ker in 41. Heth Fal contested the claim of the named heir Sor Pean in 41, took the seat, and was challenged openly by Throll Kell in 42, who won and took the seat. Throll Kell held the seat at the end of the period. The Compact’s standing fell to nothing twice, prompting Threi Cut to rise in 30 and Laehiford to rise in 37.

The Compact engaged in three wars. It declared war on the Kebarrow Compact in 27, fighting two battles at Vea Lode that killed 57 and 48 enemies, before making peace in 29. It declared war on the Wurn League in 33, fighting two battles at Hadale that killed 58 and 87 enemies, taking Hadale in 34 and finishing the Wurn League. It declared war on the Kebarrow Compact in 38, fighting two battles at Kebarrow that killed 142 and 118 enemies, taking Kebarrow in 39 and finishing the Kebarrow Compact. The Compact sent three raids, all successful: against Hadale in 24, Kebarrow in 26, and Vea Lode in 42. It suffered fourteen raids, three of which carried off plunder.

### The Meigate Covenant, 29–51

The Meigate Covenant’s twenty-three years from 29 to 51 were defined by a cycle of expansion, internal fracture, and military failure against the Griwick Compact. The power began by breaking from the Kebarrow Compact, with Raes Go taking the seat. Early efforts to project strength failed, as raids on Griwick and Laehiford were beaten off. The Covenant stabilized through marriages binding it to the Vea Lode Covenant and the Griwick Compact, and by absorbing eleven exiles who returned to take service. However, a famine at Meigate in 38 killed 61 people and left grain short, weakening the state. Raes Go lost the seat in 44 after losing an open challenge to Diweith Mound, who was himself killed in 46. Drarka Draernthun then took the seat by setting aside the claim of the named heir Tor Nathgoull.

The period ended with the Covenant exhausted and diminished. It declared war on the Griwick Compact in 47, breaking its alliance, but lost both battles at Threi Cut, suffering 12 and 10 dead respectively. The war lasted two years before peace was made. During this time, Vea Lode rose against the Covenant in 45 after its standing had fallen to nothing. The Covenant sent eight raids, four of which were beaten off and four of which carried off plunder. It suffered two raids, one of which took plunder and one of which took nothing. Hunger at Vea Lode over several years killed 6 people and caused 60 to flee. Three people were cast out, and three were courted away from the ruler. The period ended in 51 with Drarka Draernthun still holding the seat.

### The Griwick Compact, 44–51

The Griwick Compact endured eight years of violent succession and external war, defined by a rapid turnover of rulers and a persistent conflict with the Meigate Covenant. Six individuals held the seat in this period, each ruling for an average of 1.1 years. The power began with Throll Kell, who was killed in 44, and ended with Paernmel Has, who took the seat in 50 and still held it. The seat passed five times, through murder, coup, and contested claim. The Compact fought two battles against the Meigate Covenant, winning both at Threi Cut, but suffered three raids and lost one war’s momentum to exhaustion.

Lorn Waldhith had Throll Kell murdered at Griwick in 44. Tum Ga took the seat by the strongest claim but was challenged and beaten by Stonand Ker later that year. Stonand Ker seized the seat by coup and cast out Tum Ga. Lorn Waldhith’s conspiracy against Stonand Ker was uncovered after two years, and Lorn was cast out for the plot. Stonand Ker had Diweith Mound murdered at Meigate in 46. Kou Peis then had Stonand Ker murdered at Hadale in 47. The Meigate Covenant declared war and broke its alliance with the Compact. The Meigate Covenant raided Griwick, carrying off 23 grain and killing 16. The Compact defeated the Meigate Covenant at Threi Cut, with 12 dead.

Thres Thrild, the named heir, took the seat after his rival Drouldthas Stour’s claim was rejected; Drouldthas was cast out. Kou Peis’s conspiracy against Thres Thrild was uncovered after two years, and Kou Peis was executed. The Compact defeated the Meigate Covenant at Threi Cut again, with 10 dead. Raes Go challenged and beat Thres Thrild, taking the seat by coup and casting out Thres Thrild. Raes Go was challenged openly by Paernmel Has, who won and took the seat, killing Raes Go at Griwick. The Meigate Covenant raided Laehiford, which was beaten off. The two powers made peace after two years of war. The Meigate Covenant raided Hadale, carrying off 49 grain and killing 14.

### The rule of Paernmel Has over the Griwick Compact, 50–51

In year 50, Paernmel Has challenged Raes Go openly for the seat of the Griwick Compact and won, taking the position from Raes Go. Later that same year, Paernmel Has killed Raes Go at Griwick. Tor Nathgoull was raised to steward of the Griwick Compact in year 50.

The period saw internal instability and external aggression. In year 51, Visal Veill married Treild Haen at Hadale, and Paernmel Has’s child, Gim Raeldrol, was born at Kebarrow. Voudreirn Wer, Trurkeand Busi, and Ta Poveil each won Bakrei Dea away from the ruler of the Griwick Compact in consecutive years. The Griwick Compact spent 205 silver buying goodwill for the fifth time in year 51, but was not believed. In year 51, the Meigate Covenant raided Hadale, carrying off 49 grain and killing 14.

The records do not state how Paernmel Has’s rule ended, only that he held the seat through year 51.
