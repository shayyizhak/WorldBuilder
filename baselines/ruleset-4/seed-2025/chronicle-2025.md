### The Waeslefell Compact, 22–41

The Waeslefell Compact’s twenty-year span from 22 to 41 was defined by a violent succession, a protracted war with the Baesveireach Compact, and a final collapse of authority that left its ruler dead and its standing ruined. The period began with Vaell Foull holding the seat, a position he lost when Ground Raeskur had him murdered at Skomere in 24. Ground Raeskur took the seat by right of birth, but his rule was immediately challenged by internal conspiracies and external warfare. He survived an attempt on his life by Staellou Keillthae in 28, who was cast out and later declared an outlaw. Ground Raeskur himself was cast out in 32 after losing an open challenge to Fur Roundpoull.

Fur Roundpoull ruled for one year before dying in Waeslefell in 33. His death triggered a contest between Rontaen Sin and the named heir Rearn Thill. Rontaen Sin took the seat, setting aside Rearn Thill’s claim, and the Compact was divided, with Rearn Thill carrying off two places as Greater Baesveireach. Rontaen Sin’s rule ended in 36 when Nounmam Mith had him murdered at Waeslefell. Deththou Keis took the seat by right of birth, but Nounmam Mith challenged him in 37, won, and took the seat by coup, casting Deththou Keis out. Nounmam Mith held the seat until the end of the period.

The Compact’s military history was dominated by its war with the Baesveireach Compact, which lasted four years. The Compact lost one battle at Thea Seam in 26, with 36 dead, but won three others: at Thea Seam in 27 with 30 dead, and at Baesveireach in 28 and 29 with 82 and 66 dead respectively. It took Thea Seam and Baesveireach from the Baesveireach Compact. The Compact also fought the Deafil League, defeating it at Skomere in 34 with 179 dead and taking the settlement, which led to the Deafil League’s destruction. The Compact sent two raids, both carrying off plunder, and suffered nine raids, all of which were beaten off.

Internal stability eroded throughout the period. Six years of hunger at Thea Seam killed 23 people and caused 60 to flee. The Compact’s standing fell to nothing, leading Thea Seam to rise against it in 35. Nine people were courted away from its ruler. The Compact’s rule ended with Nounmam Mith still holding the seat, but the power had lost two places to Greater Baesveireach and its authority was broken.

### Free Kremoor, 28–51

Free Kremoor spent its twenty-four years from 28 to 51 as a fragile entity, born from the Deafil League and sustained by the return of exiles who repeatedly challenged its leadership. Feinnind Keitrith held the seat from the start in 28, ruling for 16 years before his rule ended when he was cast out in 44. His tenure was marked by internal violence and the defection of key figures to the Waeslefell Compact. Nounmam Mith succeeded him in 44, holding the seat for five years until he too was cast out in 49. Til Saeld took the seat in 49 and remained in power when the period ended. The power never expanded its territory, sending five raids that were all beaten off, while losing two of its own rulers to attempted murder.

Feinnind Keitrith’s long rule began with the return of exiles Staellou Keillthae, Touthseand Gourses, Vamkei Dridrill, Ground Raeskur, and Sealdvern Geitoun, who all took service with Free Kremoor. Despite this influx, internal strife erupted early. In 35, Staellou Keillthae challenged Feinnind Keitrith openly for the seat, lost, and was killed by Feinnind Keitrith at Kremoor. This violence did not stabilize the regime. In 37, Ground Raeskur abandoned Free Kremoor for the Waeslefell Compact, prompting a failed raid on Waeslefell. Later, Sealdvern Geitoun attempted to murder Feinnind Keitrith in 39 and was cast out, only to be declared an outlaw in 40 after a conspiracy was uncovered. Feinnind Keitrith himself attempted to murder Greandsam Kreir in 44, leading to his own casting out.

Nounmam Mith took the seat by election in 44, bringing Nounmam Mith’s own exile into the fold. His rule was short and ended in similar fashion. In 49, he attempted to murder Woum Teldlei, failed, and was cast out. Til Saeld, raised from Kremoor with no remaining claimant, took the seat in 49. The power’s external posture remained consistently defensive; it sent raids against Thea Seam, Fa Cut, Waeslefell, and Skomere, all of which were beaten off. No raids got through, and no plunder was carried off. The period ended with Til Saeld still holding the seat, having inherited a power that had lost two rulers to internal violence and seen seven exiles return, yet failing to secure its borders or its internal loyalty.

### The Waeslefell Compact, 42–51

The Waeslefell Compact’s decade was defined by violent succession and rapid disintegration. It began with Nounmam Mith holding the seat, a position he lost in 42 when Greandsam Kreir challenged him openly and took power. The period saw six different rulers, each holding the seat for an average of 1.5 years, with the seat passing five times. The Compact’s authority eroded from internal murder and contested claims to external rebellion, ending with Haeth Tha taking the seat in 51 while the power itself remained intact but diminished.

Greandsam Kreir ruled from 42 until his murder in 47 by Thraebaern Ramdem at Skomere. During his rule, the Compact sent one raid on Weisfaewick, carrying off 41 grain and killing 49, while beating off raids from Greater Baesveireach on Waeslefell and Skomere. After Kreir’s death, Noulldou Beivourn took the seat by setting aside the claim of the named heir Touthseand Gourses. This contest split the Compact: Touthseand Gourses carried off two places, Skomere and Thea Seam, to form the Skomere Charter. Noulldou Beivourn was cast out in 48 by Woum Teldlei, who had challenged him openly.

Woum Teldlei’s rule ended when he was killed by Feinnind Keitrith in 49. Ronith Wasnur then took the seat, setting aside the claim of the named heir Meirnhaes Kould, who was cast out. Ronith’s tenure was marked by instability; he faced an attempted murder by Gandkim Kiveald and a conspiracy by Full Sanweil, both of whom were cast out. In 50, Seithfum Mim married Til Saeld, binding the Compact to Free Kremoor. Ronith died in 51 at age 18. Haeth Tha took the seat that year, setting aside the claim of the named heir Thraebaern Ramdem, who was cast out.

The Compact suffered significant losses beyond leadership changes. A plague at Skomere in 46 and 47 killed 176 people and caused 176 others to flee. Fa Cut rose against the Compact in 48 after its standing had fallen to nothing, though Free Kremoor’s raid on Fa Cut in 51 was beaten off. The Compact’s own people murdered from within totaled one, and six people were cast out for various reasons. Two marriages tied the Compact to Free Kremoor. The period ended with Haeth Tha holding the seat, having survived the turbulent succession of the previous decade.

## Reigns

_In the order they begin._

### The rule of Til Saeld over Free Kremoor, 49–51

Til Saeld assumed the seat of Free Kremoor in 49, following the exile of Nounmam Mith. Mith had been cast out after an attempt on the life of Woum Teldlei failed and was traced to him. With no claimant remaining, Saeld was raised from Kremoor to hold the power.

The period concluded with a military failure. In 51, Free Kremoor launched a raid on Fa Cut, which was beaten off.

### The rule of Haeth Tha over the Waeslefell Compact, 51–51

In year 51, Ronith Wasnur, the ruler of the Waeslefell Compact, died in Waeslefell. Haeth Tha contested the claim of Thraebaern Ramdem, the named heir, to the seat of the Waeslefell Compact. Haeth Tha took the seat, setting aside the claim of Thraebaern Ramdem.

Thraebaern Ramdem was cast out of the Waeslefell Compact. During this same year, a raid by Free Kremoor on Fa Cut was beaten off.
