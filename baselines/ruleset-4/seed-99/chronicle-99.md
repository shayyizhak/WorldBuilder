### The War of the Peirnhewick Crown's Grudge: the Peirnhewick Crown against the Staernwefell Crown, 7–9

In year 7, the Peirnhewick Crown declared war on the Staernwefell Crown over Sa Cut. The Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Staernwefell Crown at Sa Cut, where 29 people died, and took the site from them. In year 8, the Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Staernwefell Crown at Staernwefell, where 115 people died, and took that settlement from them.

In year 9, the Peirnhewick Crown and the Staernwefell Crown made peace after 2 years, following the collapse of the Staernwefell Crown.

### The the Peirnhewick Crown Aggression: the Peirnhewick Crown against the Pistead Compact, 11–13

The Peirnhewick Crown declared war on the Pistead Compact in year 11, citing a long-standing grievance. The Compact defeated the Peirnhewick Crown at Pistead in the same year, with 64 dead. In year 12, the Compact defeated the Peirnhewick Crown again at Kraefell, with 54 dead.

The war ended in year 13 when the two powers made peace after two years, both sides exhausted. In year 14, Krullkell Skild had Beald Feithsith murdered at Pistead.

### The Staernwefell Crown, 4–9

The Staernwefell Crown endured six years of internal collapse and external defeat, beginning with a stable succession and ending in total destruction. Stealmaell Nothmi held the seat until his death in year 4, after which Faeth Paes took power by the strongest claim. The period was defined by rapid turnover among rulers and a single, fatal conflict with the Peirnhewick Crown. Five people held the seat in total, each ruling for an average of 0.8 years, with the seat passing four times. The power lost two places, Sa Cut and Staernwefell, to its rival and was finished by year 8.

Internal instability weakened the Crown before the war escalated. Faespa Futhbill challenged Faeth Paes in year 5 and won, taking the seat by coup and casting Paes out. Vopeis Foldthous then had Futhbill murdered in year 7, and Dinthei Lemmar succeeded him by the strongest claim. Vopeis Foldthous challenged Lemmar in year 8, won, and took the seat by coup, casting Lemmar out. Two people were cast out as the losing side of a coup, and one person was killed by his own people. A famine at Sa Cut in year 5 killed 7 people and left grain short by 4.

The external war began in year 7 when the Peirnhewick Crown declared war on the Staernwefell Crown for Sa Cut. The Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Staernwefell Crown at Sa Cut, where 29 died, and took the site. In year 8, the Peirnhewick Crown defeated the Staernwefell Crown at Staernwefell, where 115 died, and took that site as well. The Staernwefell Crown was finished, its landless last 6 followers scattering. A raid on Puholt in year 8 was beaten off. Peace was made in year 9 after two years of war.

### The Pistead Compact, 6–22

The Pistead Compact’s seventeen-year existence was defined by a cycle of violent succession and a persistent, ultimately fatal, conflict with the Peirnhewick Crown. The period began with a plague at Pistead that killed 363 people, followed immediately by internal purges. Vasdo Dri, the ruler, was killed by Beald Feithsith, who then took the seat by coup after defeating Krullkell Skild in an open challenge. The Compact’s early years were marked by retaliation against Peirnhewick for its raids, leading to a war that the Compact won twice, at Pistead and Kraefell, before peace was made.

Power shifted frequently among six rulers, with the seat passing five times. After Beald Feithsith was killed by Krullkell Skild, Ba Turtheill took the seat by election, only to be cast out when Dinthei Lemmar challenged him openly. Dinthei Lemmar ruled until he was killed by Krullkell Skild, who had previously lost the seat. Goth Beildbea then took the seat by election. During this time, the Compact lost Kraefell to the Second Crown of Kraefell and suffered three raids from Peirnhewick and one from the Burghers of Staernwefell. It sent out seven raids, three of which carried off plunder and four of which were beaten off.

The Compact’s end came through its war with the Peirnhewick Crown. After a new declaration of war, Peirnhewick defeated the Compact at Pistead, taking the settlement. The Compact was finished, its land lost and its last 10 followers scattering. The period ended with peace between the two powers, but the Compact itself was destroyed. Throughout its history, the Compact engaged in three battles against Peirnhewick, winning two and losing one, and saw three exiles return to service. Its rule ended with Goth Beildbea, whose house ended.

### The Burghers of Staernwefell, 13–39

The Burghers of Staernwefell existed for twenty-seven years, from 13 to 39, as a power that broke from the Peirnhewick Crown only to be destroyed by it. The period began with Duth Traellkeild taking the seat in 13, establishing a rule that lasted until his cast-out in 33. His tenure was marked by a series of failed raids against Peirnhewick, Puholt, Pistead, Sa Cut, and Nae Seam, all of which were beaten off. The power survived internal challenges, such as Vour Trouthteith’s open challenge in 30, which Duth repelled before killing his rival. The seat passed to Goth Beildbea in 33 by election after Duth’s removal. Goth’s rule was brief and violent, ending in the power’s destruction.

Goth Beildbea’s short tenure saw the Burghers of Staernwefell swear an alliance with the Second Crown of Kraefell in 29, but the long-standing grievance with the Peirnhewick Crown erupted into war in 37. The Peirnhewick Crown declared war, and the Burghers suffered two defeats at Staernwefell in 37 and 38, with 128 and 107 dead respectively. During this conflict, Goth ordered the murder of Gaedrell Meand at Pistead. Despite the loss of the seat of Staernwefell to the Peirnhewick Crown in 38, the Burghers continued to raid, sending out nine raids in total; eight were beaten off, but one against Kraefell in 38 carried off 22 grain and killed 37.

The power finished in 38 when its land was taken and its last five followers scattered. The Peirnhewick Crown and the Burghers of Staernwefell made peace in 39 after two years of war, following the collapse of the Burghers. The period saw two people hold the seat, with the seat passing once from Duth Traellkeild to Goth Beildbea. Two battles were fought, both lost to the Peirnhewick Crown. One war was begun, lasting two years. One person was cast out for attempted murder, and two exiles returned to take service. One person was courted away from the ruler. The quarrel between the Peirnhewick Crown and the Burghers of Staernwefell persisted throughout, resulting in two battles between them.

### The Second Crown of Kraefell, 15–44

The Second Crown of Kraefell existed for thirty years as a breakaway power that never secured its independence. It began in 15 when Kraefell separated from the Pistead Compact, with Neldthear Throum taking the seat. The new state faced immediate devastation, losing 323 people to plague in 17. Its early years were defined by external aggression rather than internal consolidation. The Peirnhewick Crown raided Kraefell in 25, taking 41 grain and killing 37. The Second Crown attempted to project power through six raids between 30 and 40, all of which were beaten off at Sa Cut, Nae Seam, Peirnhewick, Pistead, Puholt, and Staernwefell. These failures left the state vulnerable to counter-raids, which it suffered four times, with the Peirnhewick Crown, the Burghers of Staernwefell, and Free Pistead all taking plunder from its lands.

Internal stability collapsed in 38 when Maeld Brei challenged Neldthear Throum for the seat. Maeld Brei won the challenge and took the seat by coup, casting out Neldthear Throum. This transition was marked by the return of exiles, including Maeld Brei, Duth Traellkeild, and Thethden Steith, though Thethden Steith was later courted away by Maeld Brei. The period saw two people hold the seat, with the seat passing one time. The political instability coincided with a resumption of hostilities with the Peirnhewick Crown, which declared war in 42 over a long-standing grievance. The Second Crown fought two battles against the Peirnhewick Crown, both lost at Kraefell, resulting in 207 dead in 42 and 195 dead in 43.

The war ended the power. In 43, the Peirnhewick Crown took Kraefell from the Second Crown of Kraefell. The Second Crown of Kraefell was finished, becoming landless as its last 12 followers scattered. The Peirnhewick Crown and the Second Crown of Kraefell made peace in 44 after two years of war, following the collapse of the Second Crown of Kraefell. The period ended with the Second Crown of Kraefell destroyed, its seat lost, and its people dispersed.

### The Peirnhewick Crown, 42–51

The period from 42 to 51 was defined by the Peirnhewick Crown’s rapid expansion followed by its internal collapse. At the start, the Crown held its seat through Goth Beildbea, who had ruled since 38. The decade opened with a war against the Second Crown of Kraefell, which the Peirnhewick Crown won decisively, taking Kraefell and finishing its rival. This victory was followed by a second war against Free Pistead, which also ended in the Peirnhewick Crown’s favor, resulting in the loss of Puholt and Pistead for its enemy. The Crown’s standing fell to nothing in 47 when Nae Seam rose against it, a decline marked by the murder of its ruler, Goth Beildbea, and the subsequent seizure of the seat by Dusvoth Pe, who set aside the named heir Neldthear Throum.

The seat passed four times in these ten years, with an average tenure of 1.8 years. Dusvoth Pe held the seat until his murder by Nothmeand Trearol in 48. Nothmeand Trearol then contested the claim of the named heir Maeld Brei, winning the seat in a coup. Maeld Brei’s rule was brief; he was challenged openly by Duth Traellkeild, who took the seat and was subsequently killed by Nothmeand Trearol, who then took the seat again. Throughout this time, the Crown suffered six raids, all beaten off by Free Pistead before its collapse, and lost five places to the Sworn Men of Pistead and Free Kraefell. The quarrel between the Peirnhewick Crown and the Second Crown of Kraefell saw two battles, both won by the Crown.

By the end of 51, the Peirnhewick Crown had made peace with Free Kraefell, both sides exhausted. Nothmeand Trearol still held the seat, having taken it in a coup after killing Duth Traellkeild. The Crown’s standing had fallen from nothing to a state of exhaustion, having lost places to Free Kraefell and seen its ruler killed multiple times. Six people were killed while holding the seat, and one remained. The period ended with the seat in the hands of Nothmeand Trearol, who had won it through violence rather than primogeniture, leaving the Crown divided and its authority severely diminished.

### The Sworn Men of Pistead, 47–50

The period from 47 to 50 saw the Sworn Men of Pistead transition from a branch of the Peirnhewick Crown to an independent line under new leadership. At the start, Neldthear Throum held the seat, having separated three places from the Peirnhewick Crown in 47. His rule ended with his death in Pistead in 50.

Vi Thrinli, born in 23, returned from exile in 49 and took service with the Sworn Men of Pistead. Following Neldthear’s death later that same year, Vi Thrinli took the seat by right of birth, completing the succession.

### The rule of Nothmeand Trearol over the Peirnhewick Crown, 51–51
