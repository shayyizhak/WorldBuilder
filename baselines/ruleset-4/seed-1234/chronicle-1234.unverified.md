The Galweall League spent twenty years defined by its rivalry with the Trostead Compact, a conflict that began with raids and ended in a war of attrition. At the start, the League was bound to House Buldbei through the marriage of Paernso Feand and Trurnur Draeltheas. The period opened with the League suffering raids on Gaehollow and Themoor, while its own raid on Trostead was beaten off. Lald Neirntean consolidated power by ordering the murder of Faellwil Na at Trostead. The League’s strength appeared to grow as five exiles returned to take service, including Gall Brolllea, Frernli Peimeath, Threth Kruth, Neallseis Staend, and Kesfound Thrael. These men enabled the League to raid Geirnpei Shafts and Pellweagate, carrying off grain and killing dozens.

The dynamic shifted when Kesfound Thrael abandoned the League for the Trostead Compact, leading to a failed raid on Geirnpei Shafts that took nothing. The Trostead Compact resumed its raids on Gaehollow and Themoor, prompting a retaliatory raid by the League on Pellweagate. Lald Neirntean continued to eliminate internal threats, ordering the murders of Paepu Noth and Kesfound Thrael. In year 19, the League declared war on the Trostead Compact over a long-standing grievance. The Trostead Compact defeated the League at Geirnpei Shafts, killing 19. The war continued into year 20, where the League and its allies defeated the Trostead Compact at the same site, killing 16, and took Geirnpei Shafts from them.

The period ended with the death of the ruler and a new peace. Paernso Feand murdered Lald Neirntean, who was killed while holding the seat. Threth Kruth took the seat by election. The League and the Trostead Compact made peace after two years of war, both sides exhausted. Sealfeis Vearn married Beasre Feanwoul, binding the League to House Buldbei again. The League had fought two battles, one lost and one won, and had sent five raids, three of which carried off plunder. It had suffered four raids and taken one place. The quarrel with the Trostead Compact remained the central feature of the period.

The Trostead Compact’s nine-year existence from 22 to 30 was defined by a rapid descent from predatory aggression to total destruction. The period opened with the Compact raiding Micrag, carrying off 28 grain and killing 48, but ended with its landless followers scattering after a decisive defeat. The power’s internal stability was fragile, marked by a succession that shifted twice among three rulers, each rule ending in violence or exile. External pressures mounted as the Galweall League, which suffered a raid on Trostead in 24, turned the tables by declaring war in 29 and taking the settlement from the Compact.

Vun Doultheam held the seat from before 22 until his death in 25. His rule ended when Tha Vu had him murdered at Trostead, an act recorded as retaliation for the Compact’s earlier failed raid on Geirnpei Shafts in 23. Tha Vu’s own tenure was brief; after a conspiracy against the new ruler was uncovered in 27, Tha Vu was cast out. Besveir Maleal took the seat by right of birth in 25, but his rule was challenged openly by Del Monbirn in 27. Del Monbirn won the challenge, took the seat, and cast out Besveir Maleal. Del Monbirn’s house ended shortly after, leaving the Compact leaderless against its enemies.

The Compact’s military efforts were largely unsuccessful. Of the three raids it sent out, only the initial attack on Micrag in 22 carried off plunder; the raids on Geirnpei Shafts in 23 and Gaehollow in 28 were both beaten off. The Compact suffered one raid, on Trostead in 24 by the Galweall League. Diplomatic ties were forged in 28 when Keandtei Taegro married Thimseild Soldrind, binding the Compact to the Galweall League, but this alliance did not prevent war. In 29, the Galweall League defeated the Compact at Trostead, killing 167 people and taking the settlement. The Compact was finished, its last 7 followers scattering, and peace was made with the Galweall League in 30 after one year of war.
