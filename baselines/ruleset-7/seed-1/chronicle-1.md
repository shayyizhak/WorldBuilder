## Wars

_In the order they begin._

### The War of the Wougate Crown's Grudge: the Wougate Crown against the Reswoumark Crown, 5–7

The Wougate Crown declared war on the Reswoumark Crown in year 5 over Wei Seam. In that same year, the Wougate Crown and its allies defeated the Reswoumark Crown at Wei Seam, where 29 people died. The Wougate Crown took Wei Seam from the Reswoumark Crown as a result of the defeat.

In year 6, the Wougate Crown and its allies defeated the Reswoumark Crown at Reswoumark, with 198 dead. The Wougate Crown took Reswoumark from the Reswoumark Crown following this victory.

In year 7, the Wougate Crown and the Reswoumark Crown made peace after 2 years of fighting, following the collapse of the Reswoumark Crown.

### The House Wem Aggression: House Wem against the Wougate Crown, 22–26

House Wem declared war on the Wougate Crown in 22, citing a long-standing grievance. The Wougate Crown defeated House Wem at Thu Pits in 22, with 24 dead. The Wougate Crown defeated House Wem at Thu Pits again in 23, with 20 dead.

In 24, House Wem and its allies defeated the Wougate Crown at Thu Pits, with 16 dead. House Wem took Thu Pits from the Wougate Crown in 24. House Wem and its allies defeated the Wougate Crown at Wei Seam in 25, with 15 dead. House Wem took Wei Seam from the Wougate Crown in 25.

House Wem and the Wougate Crown made peace in 26 after 4 years, both sides exhausted.

### The Reswoumark Crown, 4–7

The Reswoumark Crown’s rule ended in the span of three years, collapsing under the weight of external conquest and internal betrayal. At the start of the period, the power held Wei Seam and Reswoumark, but its authority was already fracturing. A famine at Wei Seam in year 4 killed six people and left grain short by 2. The crisis deepened in year 5, when 13 more died and 31 abandoned the place. These losses provided the pretext for the Wougate Crown to declare war on the Reswoumark Crown for control of Wei Seam.

The Wougate Crown and its allies defeated the Reswoumark Crown at Wei Seam, killing 29 people, and took the site. The conflict moved to Reswoumark in year 6, where the Wougate Crown and its allies defeated the Reswoumark Crown again, killing 198 people and taking the settlement. Within the Reswoumark leadership, Fornhir Threi had Diska Louth murdered at Reswoumark. Diska Louth, ruler of the Reswoumark Crown, was killed by Fornhir Threi at Reswoumark. The Reswoumark Crown was finished, landless, with its last 4 followers scattering.

The period ended in year 7 with the Wougate Crown and the Reswoumark Crown making peace after 2 years, following the collapse of the Reswoumark Crown. The power that began the period holding territory was destroyed within it, its end marked by the scattering of its final followers.

### The Sworn Men of Rucrag, 22–50

The Sworn Men of Rucrag spent twenty-nine years defined by a cycle of broken alliances and violent succession. The period began with Rucrag breaking from House Wem in 22, placing Difearn Kaerheald in the seat. The new power quickly stabilized through the return of exiles, including Naenddaem Houllteind, Lea Dae, and Thrir Pondsurn, while Lea Dae’s marriage to Goul Geall bound the Sworn Men to House Wem. This stability ended in 30 when Tommarn Ri challenged and defeated Difearn Kaerheald, taking the seat. Tommarn’s rule was short-lived; he was murdered by Lea Dae in 35. Lea Dae then contested the claim of the named heir, Naenddaem Houllteind, but lost the election. Lea Dae was cast out, and Naenddaem took the seat.

Naenddaem’s tenure was brief and marked by internal violence. He attempted to murder Koldfaem Trir in 36 and was cast out for the attempt. Greandoul Krean took the seat by election. His rule saw a plague at Rucrag in 37 that killed 121 people, and he ordered the murder of Koldfaem Trir at Broldtemoor in 39. Greandoul’s own rule ended in 48 when he attempted to murder Kurnsa Bealldo and was cast out. Threihoull Thild took the seat by election. Threihoull was killed by Kurnsa Bealldo in 49, and Skove Pull took the seat by election. Skove Pull was killed by Kurnsa Bealldo in 50, and Bi Binseall took the seat by election, still holding it at the end of the period.

The power’s external relations were dominated by a quarrel with House Wem that resulted in two battles, both lost. The Sworn Men sent five raids: one against Wougate carried off plunder, while raids on Thu Pits, Wei Seam, Haelabarrow, and Broldtemoor were beaten off. They suffered one raid on Rucrag by House Wem in 47, which took nothing. The period ended with peace between House Wem and the Sworn Men after two years of war, leaving Bi Binseall in the seat.

### The Wougate Crown, 42–50

The period from 42 to 50 was defined by the collapse of the Wougate Crown’s territorial integrity and its subsequent struggle for survival through dynastic alliances. At the start, the Crown faced a war declared by House Wem over a long-standing grievance, a conflict that resulted in the loss of its capital, Wougate, and the defeat of its forces. The Crown’s standing deteriorated rapidly, losing its seat and its primary settlement within the first two years. By the end of the period, the Crown had regained neither its lost territory nor its former military strength, relying instead on a series of marriages to bind itself to House Wem and the Sworn Men of Rucrag.

The initial phase of the period saw the Wougate Crown defeated twice by House Wem at Wougate, with 180 dead in 42 and 150 dead in 43. House Wem took Wougate from the Crown in 43, and the two powers made peace after two years of war following the collapse of the Crown’s position. During this time, internal instability weakened the Crown further. Voun Woldlom, who had held the seat since 38, was cast out in 43 for an attempted murder on Lea Dae. Drithne Draend took the seat in 43 by right of birth but was also cast out in 46 after an attempt on Kurnsa Bealldo failed. Halweill Tu took the seat in 46 by right of birth and remained holding it at the end of the period.

The Crown’s military efforts during the remainder of the period were uniformly unsuccessful. It sent three raids, all of which were beaten off: against Thu Pits in 45, Wei Seam in 49, and Wougate in 50. It suffered one raid in 42 by the Sworn Men of Rucrag at Wougate, which carried off 53 grain and killed 27. To secure its position, the Crown entered three marriages. Drithne Draend married Feald Keim in 46, binding the Crown to House Wem. Halweill Tu married Threihoull Thild in 48, binding the Crown to the Sworn Men of Rucrag. Steaskaes Ve married Bi Binseall in 50, further binding the Crown to the Sworn Men of Rucrag. The quarrel between the Wougate Crown and House Wem persisted, marked by two battles between them.

### The rule of Greandoul Krean over the Second Crown of Reswoumark, 51–51

In year 51, House Wem declared war on the Second Crown of Reswoumark over a long-standing grievance, causing trade between the two powers to break down. Within the Crown, Voun Woldlom’s attempt on the life of Kurnsa Bealldo failed and was traced back to him. As a result of this attempted murder, Voun Woldlom was cast out of the Second Crown of Reswoumark.

House Wem defeated the Second Crown of Reswoumark at Haelabarrow, where 219 people died. Following the vacancy created by the casting out of Voun Woldlom, Greandoul Krean took the seat of the Second Crown of Reswoumark by right of birth.
