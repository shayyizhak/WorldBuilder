# A history of the Rendfeall Vale

Seed 1234, years 1–51. Every name, date and figure below is drawn from the simulation itself.


## Wars

_In the order they begin._

### The Long Quarrel over Pei Delve: the Trostead Compact against House Buldbei, 9–11

The Trostead Compact declared war on House Buldbei in year 9, seeking control of Pei Delve. House Buldbei and its allies defeated the Compact at that site later the same year, with 23 dead. The Compact suffered another defeat at Pei Delve in year 10, where 18 died.

The conflict lasted two years. In year 11, the Trostead Compact and House Buldbei made peace, both sides exhausted.

### The War of the Galweall League's Grudge: the Galweall League against the Trostead Compact, 19–21

The Galweall League declared war on the Trostead Compact in 19, citing a long-standing grievance. The Trostead Compact defeated the Galweall League at Geirnpei Shafts in the same year, with 19 dead. In 20, Lald Neirntean had Kesfound Thrael murdered at Themoor. Later that year, the Galweall League and its allies defeated the Trostead Compact at Geirnpei Shafts, with 16 dead. The Galweall League took Geirnpei Shafts from the Trostead Compact in 20.

The war ended in 21 when the Galweall League and the Trostead Compact made peace after 2 years, both sides exhausted.

### The Rising of Pellweagate, 16–37

The Rising of Pellweagate spent its twenty-two years in a state of collapse, defined by repeated failure and internal fracture. It began in year 16 when Veith Hei took the seat after Pellweagate broke from the Trostead Compact. The period was marked by four years of hunger that killed 173 people, six raids that were all beaten off, and a succession that shifted from one ruler to another. The power ended in year 37 not with a restoration, but with its destruction by the Galweall League, which had defeated it in battle and taken its land.

Veith Hei held the seat for sixteen years, during which the Rising suffered three famines and launched raids that failed against Trostead, Gaehollow, Geirnpei Shafts, Themoor, and Pei Delve. In year 32, Find Bound challenged Veith Hei openly for the seat, won, and cast him out. Find Bound’s rule was brief; he took the seat in year 32, but his house ended in year 37. During this time, three exiles returned to serve the Rising: Beasre Feanwoul and Find Bound in year 26, and Besveir Maleal in year 33. Find Bound married Draemo Grirn in year 27, binding the Rising to the Trostead Compact, and he won Beasre Feanwoul away from Veith Hei in year 30.

The final years saw the Rising drawn into war with the Galweall League. The League declared war in year 36 over a long-standing grievance, broke its alliance with the Rising, and defeated it at Pellweagate, where 136 died. The League took Pellweagate from the Rising in year 36, and the Rising was finished, its last four followers scattering. The Galweall League and the Rising made peace in year 37 after one year of war. The Rising had fought one battle, lost it, and lost its only place.

### The Galweall League, 42–51

The Galweall League’s decade was defined by a rapid succession of rulers and a collapse of internal stability. At the start of 42, the League held Micrag and stood in opposition to House Buldbei. The period opened with a coup by Keandtei Taegro, who took the seat from Lan Thraestun. This internal shift coincided with a war against House Buldbei, which the League won at Micrag, taking the settlement and ending House Buldbei’s existence. However, the League’s standing fell to nothing by the end of 42, allowing Pei Delve to rise against it. The period ended in 51 with Greimfourn Goul holding the seat, having killed Mound Sith in an open challenge.

The seat passed seven times between eight people, with an average tenure of 1.1 years. Five rulers were killed and two were cast out. Keandtei Taegro ruled for one year before being murdered by Del Monbirn. Veith Hei took the seat by election, setting aside the claim of Haeldkeind Nen, but was cast out after losing an open challenge to Nond Mea. Nond Mea’s rule ended when he was killed by Rusund Gilteil, who had challenged him openly. Rusund Gilteil was then murdered by Was Leithwon. Varnnael Wansen took the seat by election, setting aside Peansith Rae, but was killed by Rend Greall. Mound Sith took the seat by election after Wansen’s death.

Internal violence was the dominant feature of the period. Five people were murdered from within the League’s own ranks. Del Monbirn was cast out for conspiracy, as was Vaswei Grand. Was Leithwon was cast out for conspiracy after his murder of Rusund Gilteil. The League’s cohesion was further eroded by fifteen instances of people being courted away from its rulers, including multiple attempts to win Homraell Leill and Guthwaell Wound. Seven people were cast out in total: three for conspiracy, two for losing a claim, one for the losing side of a coup, and one for the losing side of an open challenge. The League fought one battle, which it won, and took one place, Micrag, but suffered no defeats in combat.

### House Buldbei, 43–43

The period of House Buldbei’s existence in year 43 was defined by its abrupt termination. The house, which had operated under a succession by strongest, ended in that same year. Its collapse was not a gradual decline but a singular event that concluded its rule.

The end of House Buldbei coincided with a diplomatic shift in the region. In 43, the Galweall League and House Buldbei made peace. This agreement followed the collapse of House Buldbei, marking the final interaction between the two powers. The peace settlement served as the formal closure to the house’s presence.

### The rule of Greimfourn Goul over the Galweall League, 51–51

Greimfourn Goul took the seat of the Galweall League in 51 by defeating Mound Sith in an open challenge. The previous ruler, Mound Sith, was killed by Goul at Themoor later that same year.

The Galweall League spent 140 silver to buy back goodwill.

Goul’s rule ended in 51.
