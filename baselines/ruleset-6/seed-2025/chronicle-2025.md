# A history of the Foldkal Vale

Seed 2025, years 1–51. Every name, date and figure below is drawn from the simulation itself.


## Wars

_In the order they begin._

### The Thea Seam War: the Waeslefell Compact against the Baesveireach Compact, 4–10

The war began in year 4 when the Waeslefell Compact declared war on the Baesveireach Compact for control of Thea Seam. In that same year, the Baesveireach Compact and its allies defeated the Waeslefell Compact at Thea Seam, leaving 26 dead. The conflict continued at the same site through year 9, with the Baesveireach Compact and its allies defeating the Waeslefell Compact in each subsequent year. The casualties in these engagements decreased over time: 20 dead in year 5, 13 in year 6, 11 in year 7, 9 in year 8, and 8 in year 9.

The fighting ended in year 10 when the Waeslefell Compact and the Baesveireach Compact made peace after six years of war. Both sides were exhausted by the end of the conflict.

### The the Waeslefell Compact Aggression: the Waeslefell Compact against the Baesveireach Compact, 26–30

In 26, the Waeslefell Compact declared war on the Baesveireach Compact over a long-standing grievance. The Baesveireach Compact and its allies defeated the Waeslefell Compact at Thea Seam in the same year, with 36 dead. In 27, the Waeslefell Compact defeated the Baesveireach Compact at Thea Seam, with 30 dead, and took the site from the Baesveireach Compact.

In 28, the Waeslefell Compact defeated the Baesveireach Compact at Baesveireach, with 82 dead. In 29, Ground Raeskur had Steapall Faedild murdered at Baesveireach. Later that year, the Waeslefell Compact defeated the Baesveireach Compact at Baesveireach, with 66 dead, and took the settlement from the Baesveireach Compact.

In 30, the Waeslefell Compact and the Baesveireach Compact made peace after 4 years, following the collapse of the Baesveireach Compact.


## Powers

_In the order they begin._

### The Deafil League, 2–35

The Deafil League spent thirty-four years bound to the Baesveireach Compact through four marriages, a period of enforced alignment that ended in ruin. The League’s standing deteriorated from a position of contractual obligation to one of isolation, culminating in its destruction by the Waeslefell Compact. The power began with its ruler, Feasleith Husbill, holding the seat in Skomere, and ended with its last followers scattered and its lands lost.

Feasleith Husbill died in 11, triggering a succession dispute that Steapall Faedild won by election, casting out the named heir Bage Busba. Faedild’s rule was challenged openly by Fur Roundpoull in 23, who took the seat and cast out Faedild. Fur Roundpoull’s tenure was marked by internal instability; a conspiracy by Vaepe Lirnkour was uncovered in 27, leading to Lirnkour’s execution. In the same year, Sealdvern Geitoun challenged and defeated Fur Roundpoull in a coup, taking the seat and casting out the former ruler. Geitoun’s rule was brief and violent; an attempt on Ground Raeskur in 29 led to Geitoun’s own expulsion for attempted murder. Deththou Keis then won the seat by election, casting out the named heir Touthseand Gourses. Keis’s house ended in 35, concluding the period.

The League’s military posture was defined by retaliation against the Waeslefell Compact. After the Compact raided Kremoor in 8, the League raided Waeslefell, carrying off 24 grain and killing 59. When the Compact raided Skomere in 9, the League raided Weisfaewick, carrying off 20 grain and killing 49. Subsequent raids on Fa Cut in 10 got through but took nothing. The League’s offensive capacity waned; its raids on Waeslefell in 17, Weisfaewick in 18, and Fa Cut in 19 were all beaten off. By 30 and 31, raids on Baesveireach and Thea Seam were also beaten off. The League suffered four raids from the Compact, all carrying off plunder. In 28, Kremoor broke from the League as Free Kremoor. In 34, the Waeslefell Compact declared war, defeated the League at Skomere with 179 dead, and took the settlement. The League was finished, its land lost and its followers scattered.

### The Baesveireach Compact, 4–30

The Baesveireach Compact’s twenty-seven years were defined by a war with the Waeslefell Compact that lasted ten years and ended in the loss of its territory. The conflict began in year 4 when Waeslefell declared war for Thea Seam and broke its alliance with Baesveireach. Baesveireach and its allies won seven successive battles at Thea Seam between years 4 and 9, killing 26, 20, 13, 11, 9, and 8 opponents respectively. Raids launched by Baesveireach against Waeslefell settlements in years 6, 7, 8, and 15 were mostly beaten off, though one raid in year 6 carried off 30 grain and killed 59. Peace was made in year 10 after six years of fighting.

Internal instability weakened the Compact during the peace. Sildbeld Bouthwoun died in year 13, and Kramboul Dend took the seat by setting aside the named heir Fur Roundpoull, who was cast out. Kramboul died in year 18, and Bage Busba took the seat by the strongest claim. The Compact’s standing fell very low by year 16, when Thea Seam rose against it. A plague broke out in Baesveireach in year 21, killing 213 people, followed by 179 deaths and 224 fleees in year 22, and 114 deaths and 143 fleees in year 23. Hunger at Thea Seam also claimed lives during this period.

War resumed in year 26 when Waeslefell declared war over a long-standing grievance. Baesveireach lost three battles in years 27, 28, and 29, losing Thea Seam and Baesveireach to Waeslefell. Steapall Faedild murdered Bage Busba in year 28 and took the seat by setting aside the named heir Drourn Nethi, who was cast out. Steapall was killed by Ground Raeskur in year 29. The period ended in year 30 with peace between the powers after the collapse of the Baesveireach Compact.

### Free Kremoor, 28–51

Free Kremoor began in year 28 as a breakaway from the Deafil League, with Feinnind Keitrith taking the seat. The period lasted 24 years, during which the power maintained an alliance with the Deafil League and later with Greater Baesveireach. It sent out five raids, all of which were beaten off. Three people held the seat, passing it twice. Feinnind Keitrith held the seat for most of the period before being cast out. Nounmam Mith took the seat by election after Keitrith’s fall, only to be cast out himself. Til Saeld took the seat in year 49 and remained there when the period ended.

Feinnind Keitrith’s rule was marked by internal violence and the return of exiles. Seven exiles returned to take service with Free Kremoor during these years. Staellou Keillthae challenged Keitrith openly in year 35, lost, and was killed by Keitrith at Kremoor. Sealdvern Geitoun attempted to murder Keitrith in year 39 and was cast out. Keitrith himself attempted to murder Greandsam Kreir in year 44 and was cast out. Ground Raeskur and Touthseand Gourses abandoned Free Kremoor for the Waeslefell Compact in years 37 and 40 respectively.

Nounmam Mith’s rule was brief and ended in violence. He took the seat in year 44 by election. In year 49, he attempted to murder Woum Teldlei and was cast out. Til Saeld took the seat in year 49, raised from Kremoor with no claimant remaining. Sealdvern Geitoun, already gone, was declared outlaw in year 40 for conspiracy against the seat. The period ended with Til Saeld still holding the seat.

### The Waeslefell Compact, 42–51

The Waeslefell Compact endured a decade of violent instability, marked by six rapid successions and the loss of its territorial integrity. At the start of the period, the seat was held by Nounmam Mith, who had ruled since 37. In 42, Greandsam Kreir challenged him openly, won, and took the seat, casting Mith out. The Compact’s standing was already fragile; in the same year, it raided Weisfaewick, carrying off 41 grain and killing 49, but its own raid on Waeslefell by Greater Baesveireach was beaten off. The period was defined by internal purges and external pressures that eroded its power.

The seat passed five times in ten years, with rulers lasting an average of 1.5 years. Kreir ruled until 47, when he was murdered at Skomere by Thraebaern Ramdem. Sealdvern Geitoun, the steward, was also killed that year by Feinnind Keitrith. Noulldou Beivourn then took the seat, setting aside the claim of the named heir Touthseand Gourses. This contest divided the Compact: Gourses carried off two places, Skomere and Thea Seam, to form the Skomere Charter. Beivourn’s rule ended in 48 when Woum Teldlei challenged him openly, won, and cast him out. Fa Cut rose against the Compact that year, its standing having fallen to nothing.

Teldlei was killed in 49 by Feinnind Keitrith. Ronith Wasnur took the seat, setting aside the claim of the named heir Meirnhaes Kould, who was cast out. Wasnur’s rule was plagued by conspiracies; Gandkim Kiveald and Full Sanweil were both cast out for plotting against him. In 50, Seithfum Mim married Til Saeld, binding the Compact to Free Kremoor. Wasnur died in 51 at age 18. Haeth Tha took the seat, setting aside the claim of the named heir Thraebaern Ramdem, who was cast out. The Compact survived the period, but it had lost two places, suffered two years of plague at Skomere that killed 176 people, and seen its authority shattered by internal strife.

### The Skomere Charter, 47–51

The Skomere Charter’s tenure from 47 to 51 was defined by its precarious origin and the gradual reintegration of exiled figures into its service. The period began with the division of the Waeslefell Compact, an event that allowed Touthseand Gourses to carry off two places and establish the Skomere Charter. This succession by primogeniture marked the charter’s foundation, rooted in the fragmentation of its predecessor rather than a conquest of its own.

Touthseand Gourses held the seat through the early years of the charter’s existence. The stability of his rule was tested not by internal rebellion but by the return of those previously cast out. In 50, Nounmam Mith returned from exile and took service with the Skomere Charter, signaling a shift in the available personnel supporting the seat.

The trend of reintegration continued into the final year of the period. In 51, Meirnhaes Kould also returned from exile and took service with the Skomere Charter. The period ended with the charter still under Touthseand Gourses’ hold, having absorbed two exiled figures into its service without any recorded change in the holder of the seat.


## Reigns

_In the order they begin._

### The rule of Til Saeld over Free Kremoor, 49–51

Til Saeld assumed the seat of Free Kremoor in 49, following the exile of Nounmam Mith. Mith had been cast out after an attempt on the life of Woum Teldlei failed and was traced to him. With no claimant remaining, Saeld was raised from Kremoor to take the position.

The period ended in 51 when Free Kremoor’s raid on Fa Cut was beaten off.

### The rule of Haeth Tha over the Waeslefell Compact, 51–51

Haeth Tha took the seat of the Waeslefell Compact in 51, setting aside the claim of the named heir Thraebaern Ramdem. This succession followed the death of the previous ruler, Ronith Wasnur, in Waeslefell earlier that same year. The contest was decided by primogeniture, but Haeth Tha’s challenge succeeded, and Thraebaern Ramdem was cast out of the Waeslefell Compact as the losing claimant.

The period was brief, ending in 51 with Haeth Tha still holding the seat. No other significant events befell the Waeslefell Compact during this time.
