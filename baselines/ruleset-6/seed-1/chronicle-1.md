# A history of the Naesdeind Vale

Seed 1, years 1–51. Every name, date and figure below is drawn from the simulation itself.


## Wars

_In the order they begin._

### The War of the Wougate Crown's Grudge: the Wougate Crown against the Reswoumark Crown, 5–7

The Wougate Crown declared war on the Reswoumark Crown in year 5 over Wei Seam. In that same year, the Wougate Crown and its allies defeated the Reswoumark Crown at Wei Seam, where 29 people died. The Wougate Crown took Wei Seam from the Reswoumark Crown.

In year 6, the Wougate Crown and its allies defeated the Reswoumark Crown at Reswoumark, where 198 people died. The Wougate Crown took Reswoumark from the Reswoumark Crown.

In year 7, the Wougate Crown and the Reswoumark Crown made peace after 2 years, following the collapse of the Reswoumark Crown.

### The House Wem Aggression: House Wem against the Wougate Crown, 22–26

House Wem declared war on the Wougate Crown in 22, citing a long-standing grievance. The Wougate Crown defeated House Wem at Thu Pits in 22, with 24 dead. The Wougate Crown defeated House Wem at Thu Pits again in 23, with 20 dead.

In 24, House Wem and its allies defeated the Wougate Crown at Thu Pits, with 16 dead. House Wem took Thu Pits from the Wougate Crown in 24. In 25, House Wem and its allies defeated the Wougate Crown at Wei Seam, with 15 dead. House Wem took Wei Seam from the Wougate Crown in 25.

House Wem and the Wougate Crown made peace in 26 after 4 years, both sides exhausted.


## Powers

_In the order they begin._

### The Wougate Crown, 2–21

The Wougate Crown’s twenty-year rule, spanning years 2 to 21, began with territorial expansion and ended in fragmentation. It started by occupying Thu Pits in year 2 and ended with Lea Dae taking the seat while the power was divided. The period was defined by a single war against the Reswoumark Crown, which lasted two years and resulted in the destruction of that rival power. Within the Wougate Crown itself, five people held the seat, with the position changing hands four times through death, open challenge, and murder. The power’s standing deteriorated from holding territory to losing it, as places rose against it when its authority fell.

The Wougate Crown secured its early years through military victory and marriage alliances. It declared war on the Reswoumark Crown in year 5 for Wei Seam and defeated it at Wei Seam and Reswoumark in years 5 and 6, taking both places. The Reswoumark Crown was finished in year 6, and peace was made in year 7. Marriages in years 3 and 5 bound the Wougate Crown to House Wem. The seat passed to Sus Waernthaeld, who died in year 12. Thris Pou took the seat by right of birth in year 12 but was challenged and cast out by Krim Groum in year 13. Krim Groum was himself challenged and cast out by Deathder Grun in year 14.

Internal instability and external raids marked the later years. The Wougate Crown sent two raids, to Broldtemoor in year 13 and Rucrag in year 15, carrying off plunder. It suffered seven raids from House Wem, all of which were beaten off except those in years 10 and 11, which carried off plunder. Hunger and sickness killed 450 people over ten years. Haelabarrow rose against the Wougate Crown in year 12, when its standing had fallen very low, and Wei Seam rose in year 19, when its standing had fallen to nothing. Naenddaem Houllteind returned from exile in year 16 but was cast out in year 20 for conspiracy. Deathder Grun was murdered in year 21 by Thrir Pondsurn. Lea Dae took the seat in year 21, setting aside the claim of Skobas Frul. The Wougate Crown was divided, with Skobas Frul taking two places as the Second Crown of Reswoumark.

### The Reswoumark Crown, 4–7

The Reswoumark Crown’s tenure ended in ruin, its authority dismantled by external conquest and internal betrayal. The period began with the power holding Wei Seam, a site already suffering from famine that claimed six lives and left grain supplies critically short. The Wougate Crown declared war on the Reswoumark Crown over this territory, initiating a campaign that would strip the Reswoumark Crown of its lands and followers.

In year five, the Wougate Crown and its allies defeated the Reswoumark Crown at Wei Seam, killing twenty-nine combatants and taking the site. The conflict moved to the Reswoumark Crown’s seat in year six, where the power suffered a devastating defeat at Reswoumark, with 198 dead. The Wougate Crown took Reswoumark from the Reswoumark Crown, leaving the power finished and landless as its last four followers scattered.

Internal violence accompanied the external collapse. In year six, Fornhir Threi had Diska Louth, the ruler of the Reswoumark Crown, murdered at Reswoumark. The records link this killing to the earlier famine at Wei Seam. The Wougate Crown and the Reswoumark Crown made peace in year seven, two years after the collapse of the Reswoumark Crown, marking the end of the period.

### House Wem, 9–28

House Wem’s twenty-year span from 9 to 28 was defined by internal collapse and a protracted, exhausting war with the Wougate Crown. The period began with the house already weakened by the exile of Naenddaem Houllteind, who had been cast out in 9 for attempted murder and declared an outlaw after his conspiracy was uncovered. His return to service was short-lived; he was murdered in 15 by Wealdaern Leasha, who then took the seat by election. This act of internal violence marked the start of a pattern where the seat passed six times among seven rulers, with five of those rulers killed in their own halls. The house’s standing eroded steadily, first falling very low when Rucrag rose against it in 21, and then falling to nothing when Wei Seam followed suit in 27.

The external conflict with the Wougate Crown dominated the latter half of the period. House Wem declared war on the Wougate Crown in 22 over a long-standing grievance, breaking its alliance with them. The war lasted four years and saw four battles fought between the two powers. House Wem lost two engagements at Thu Pits, in 22 with 24 dead and in 23 with 20 dead, while winning two others at Thu Pits in 24 with 16 dead and at Wei Seam in 25 with 15 dead. During this time, the house also lost Rucrag in 22, which broke away as the Sworn Men of Rucrag, and took Thu Pits and Wei Seam from the Wougate Crown in 24 and 25 respectively. The war ended in 26 with both sides exhausted and peace made.

Internal instability continued to plague the house throughout the conflict. After the death of Goul Geall in 26, Rein Ga took the seat by election but was killed in 27. Kraeth Weageir then took the seat by setting aside the claim of the named heir Greandoul Krean, who was cast out. Kraeth Weageir remained in power at the end of the period. The house had sent out seven raids, two of which carried off plunder, and suffered two raids from the Wougate Crown. Four people were murdered from within, and four were cast out. Three marriages tied the house to other powers, binding it to the Wougate Crown twice and to the Sworn Men of Rucrag once. The period ended with House Wem still holding the seat, but its standing had fallen to nothing, and it had survived a war that had drained its resources and broken its alliances.

### The Second Crown of Reswoumark, 21–51

The period from 21 to 51 saw the Second Crown of Reswoumark rise from a breakaway of the Wougate Crown to a power that ended in military defeat. Skobas Frul established the new seat in 21 by taking two places from the Wougate Crown. The early years were marked by disaster at Haelabarrow, where plague killed 304 people in 24 and another 96 in 25, with 200 fleeing. Skobas died in 30, and Mith Thres took the seat. Mith’s rule ended in 40 when he was cast out for an attempted murder on Lea Dae. Difearn Kaerheald, the named heir, took the seat, but Kurnsa Bealldo contested the claim. Difearn kept the seat, Kurnsa was cast out, and then Difearn was killed by Kurnsa in 42.

Thraellgul Thalgin took the seat by right of birth in 42. His rule was short; he was cast out in 47 for an attempted murder on Kurnsa. Voun Woldlom took the seat by right of birth in 47. Voun’s rule ended in 51 when he was cast out for an attempted murder on Kurnsa. Greandoul Krean took the seat by right of birth in 51. The power sent out four raids, all against House Wem, and all were beaten off. It suffered three raids: one on Reswoumark in 36, one on Haelabarrow in 38, and one on Haelabarrow in 40.

In 42, Haelabarrow rose against the Second Crown of Reswoumark, whose standing had fallen to nothing. In 51, House Wem declared war on the Second Crown of Reswoumark over a long-standing grievance. House Wem defeated the Second Crown of Reswoumark at Haelabarrow in 51, with 219 dead. The period ended in 51 with Greandoul Krean still holding the seat. Six people held the seat, with an average of 5.0 years each and a median of 4.5. The seat passed from one person to another five times inside this period. Three were cast out, one died, one was killed, and one was still holding. One battle was fought, which was lost. One war was begun. Five exiles returned. Two years of hunger or sickness killed 400 in all. One place rose against it.

### The Sworn Men of Rucrag, 22–50

The Sworn Men of Rucrag spent twenty-nine years defined by a fragile independence and a series of short, contested rules. Difearn Kaerheald took the seat in 22 when Rucrag broke from House Wem, but his tenure ended in 30 after Tommarn Ri challenged him openly and won. The power’s stability was further eroded by internal violence; Lea Dae had Tommarn Ri murdered in 35, yet Naenddaem Houllteind retained the seat through election, casting Lea Dae out. The seat passed six times in this period, with seven people holding it for an average of four years each.

Alliances shifted rapidly as marriages bound the Sworn Men to House Wem in 26 and the Wougate Crown in 27. These ties did not prevent conflict. The Sworn Men sent five raids, four of which were beaten off, including attempts on Thu Pits, Wei Seam, Haelabarrow, and Broldtemoor. Only the raid on Wougate in 42 carried off plunder. Conversely, House Wem raided Rucrag in 47, taking nothing. The period saw one war begun in 48, lasting two years, during which the Sworn Men lost two battles at Rucrag to House Wem, suffering 178 dead in 48 and 148 in 49.

The end of the period was marked by rapid turnover and exhaustion. Greandoul Krean took the seat in 36 but was cast out in 48 after an attempt on Kurnsa Bealldo failed. Threihoull Thild succeeded him but was killed by Kurnsa Bealldo in 49. Skove Pull took the seat in 49 and was also killed by Kurnsa Bealldo in 50. Bi Binseall took the seat in 50, still holding it at the end of the period. The war ended in peace after both sides were exhausted, leaving the Sworn Men with a legacy of lost battles and internal purges, including the murder of Koldfaem Trir and the plague that killed 121 at Rucrag in 37.

### The Wougate Crown, 22–41

The period from 22 to 41 saw the Wougate Crown endure a war with House Wem that lasted four years, lose two strategic places, and suffer a plague that killed 290 people. Seven individuals held the seat during these twenty years, with the power passing between them six times. The conflict with House Wem defined the early years, resulting in two victories for the Crown and two defeats that cost it Thu Pits and Wei Seam. Internal instability followed, marked by three castings out for attempted murder and two murders of rulers by their own people. The period ended with Voun Woldlom holding the seat, having bound the Crown to the Second Crown of Reswoumark through marriage.

In 22, House Wem declared war on the Wougate Crown and broke its alliance. Thrir Pondsurn challenged Lea Dae for the seat, won, and took the seat by coup, casting Lea Dae out. The Crown defeated House Wem at Thu Pits, killing 24. Thrir Pondsurn’s attempt on Tin Kaes failed and was traced back, leading to his casting out in 23. The Crown defeated House Wem again at Thu Pits, killing 20. Tommarn Ri contested the claim of Haepin Maerba, the named heir, and took the seat, setting aside Haepin Maerba’s claim and casting him out. In 24, Tommarn Ri’s attempt on Tin Kaes failed and was traced back, leading to his casting out. House Wem and its allies defeated the Crown at Thu Pits, killing 16, and took Thu Pits. Sillmaem Freith took the seat by right of birth.

Sillmaem Freith had Tin Kaes murdered at Broldtemoor in 25. House Wem and its allies defeated the Crown at Wei Seam, killing 15, and took Wei Seam. Peace was made after four years of war. Kros Ranu’s conspiracy against Sillmaem Freith was uncovered, and Kros Ranu was put to death. Sillmaem Freith had Goul Geall murdered at Broldtemoor in 26 and Rein Ga murdered at Broldtemoor in 27. A plague broke out at Wougate in 28, killing 95. In 29, the plague killed 195 more and 187 fled. The Crown’s raid on Thu Pits was beaten off. The Crown and the Second Crown of Reswoumark swore an alliance.

Kurnsa Bealldo had Sillmaem Freith murdered at Wougate in 31, killing him. Neildpoun Porn took the seat by right of birth. Voun Woldlom married Mith Thres in 32, binding the Crown to the Second Crown of Reswoumark. The Crown’s raids on Wei Seam in 32 and Broldtemoor in 33 were beaten off. Kurnsa Bealldo’s conspiracy against Neildpoun Porn was uncovered in 34, and Kurnsa Bealldo was cast out. Thraellgul Thalgin challenged Neildpoun Porn openly, won, and took the seat in 35, killing Neildpoun Porn. Thraellgul Thalgin married Naerream Thrurei in 36, binding the Crown to House Wem.

Thraellgul Thalgin’s attempt on Koldfaem Trir failed and was traced back in 38, leading to his casting out. House Wem raided Wougate, carrying off 33 grain and killing 28. Voun Woldlom took the seat by right of birth. The period ended with Voun Woldlom holding the seat. The Crown had fought four battles against House Wem, winning two and losing two. It sent three raids, all beaten off, and suffered one raid. Three people were murdered from within, and six were cast out. The quarrel with House Wem persisted through battles and raids.

### The rule of Greandoul Krean over the Second Crown of Reswoumark, 51–51

In year 51, House Wem declared war on the Second Crown of Reswoumark over a long-standing grievance, causing trade between the two powers to break down. Within the Crown, Voun Woldlom’s attempt on the life of Kurnsa Bealldo failed and was traced back to him. As a result of this attempted murder, Voun Woldlom was cast out of the Second Crown of Reswoumark.

The war continued with House Wem defeating the Second Crown of Reswoumark at Haelabarrow, where 219 people died. Following the vacancy created by the exile of Voun Woldlom, Greandoul Krean took the seat of the Second Crown of Reswoumark by right of birth.
