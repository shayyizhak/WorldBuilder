## House Wem, 29–48

House Wem spent twenty years in a state of violent recovery, rising from a standing that had fallen very low to dominate its neighbors through a series of decisive wars. The period began with the house weakened, evidenced by Thu Pits rising against it in 34, but ended with House Wem holding the seat of Kurnsa Bealldo and having taken Wougate from the Wougate Crown. Five people held the seat during these years, each ruling for an average of 3.8 years, with the seat passing four times. The house’s internal stability was fragile, marked by four murders committed by its own people and three instances of individuals being courted away from its ruler, yet it maintained external strength, winning all three battles it fought.

Sir Tintoull took the seat in 30 by challenging Kraeth Weageir openly and killing him at Broldtemoor. His rule was short and violent, ending when Koldfaem Trir had him murdered at Broldtemoor in 35. Koldfaem Trir then took the seat by election. He ruled for four years before being killed by Greandoul Krean at Broldtemoor in 39. Lea Dae took the seat by election in 39 after Koldfaem Trir’s death. Lea Dae’s tenure was also cut short; Kurnsa Bealldo had Lea Dae murdered at Broldtemoor in 44. Kurnsa Bealldo took the seat by election in 44 and held it until the end of the period. Naerream Thrurei challenged Kurnsa Bealldo openly in 45, lost, and was subsequently killed by Kurnsa Bealldo at Broldtemoor.

The house’s external posture shifted from defense to aggression. It suffered eleven raids, all beaten off, between 29 and 46. In 36, House Wem raided Reswoumark, carrying off 59 grain and killing 22, which triggered a raid by the Second Crown of Reswoumark on Thu Pits that was beaten off. In 38, it raided Wougate, carrying off 33 grain and killing 28. In 40, it raided Haelabarrow, carrying off 21 grain and killing 35. In 47, it raided Rucrag, killing 27 but taking nothing. The house began two wars. In 42, it declared war on the Wougate Crown over a long-standing grievance and broke its alliance with them. It defeated the Wougate Crown at Wougate in 42 with 180 dead and again in 43 with 150 dead, taking Wougate from the Wougate Crown in 43. Peace was made in 44 after the collapse of the Wougate Crown. In 48, House Wem declared war on the Sworn Men of Rucrag over a long-standing grievance, broke its alliance with them, and defeated them at Rucrag with 178 dead.

The period from 42 to 50 was defined by the collapse of the Wougate Crown’s territorial integrity and its subsequent struggle for survival through dynastic alliances. At the start, the Crown held the seat of Wougate, but House Wem’s declaration of war and breach of alliance in 42 triggered a rapid loss of ground. Two battles were fought against House Wem, both lost at Wougate, resulting in 180 dead in 42 and 150 dead in 43. By 43, House Wem had taken Wougate from the Crown, a place lost in these years. The Crown’s military efforts were uniformly unsuccessful; three raids sent out in 45, 49, and 50 were all beaten off, and it suffered one raid in 42 by the Sworn Men of Rucrag, which carried off 53 grain and killed 27.

The internal stability of the Crown was equally fragile, marked by two castings out for attempted murder. Voun Woldlom, who had held the seat since 38, was cast out in 43 after an attempt on Lea Dae failed. Drithne Draend took the seat by right of birth in 43 but was cast out in 46 after an attempt on Kurnsa Bealldo failed. Halweill Tu took the seat by right of birth in 46 and remained holding it at the end of the period. The seat passed from one person to another two times inside this period. The Crown’s standing was further shaped by three marriages tying it to other powers: to House Wem in 46, and to the Sworn Men of Rucrag in 48 and 50. These unions did not prevent the loss of Wougate or the failure of its raids, but they linked the Crown to the powers that had defeated or raided it.

The quarrel between the Wougate Crown and House Wem persisted throughout the nine years, resulting in two battles between them. Peace was made between them in 44 after two years of war, following the collapse of the Wougate Crown’s hold on Wougate. The Crown’s rule ended not with its destruction, but with a shift in its territorial base and its reliance on external alliances. Halweill Tu still held the seat at the end of the period, but the Crown had lost its primary seat and failed in all its military initiatives. The period ended in 50 with the Crown bound by marriage to its former enemies and raiders, its military reputation diminished by three failed raids and two lost battles.

House Wem entered the period as a victor, having defeated the Sworn Men of Rucrag at Rucrag in year 49. The conflict with Rucrag lasted two years, ending in peace in year 50 when both sides were exhausted. During this time, Kurnsa Bealldo orchestrated the murder of Threihoull Thild at Rucrag in year 49, and later had Skove Pull murdered there in year 50. These acts of violence occurred while House Wem was engaged in its war with Rucrag, which concluded with a peace treaty.

In year 51, House Wem declared war on the Second Crown of Reswoumark over a long-standing grievance. The war ended quickly with House Wem defeating the Second Crown of Reswoumark at Haelabarrow, where 219 people died. This victory followed the declaration made earlier in the same year. The period began with House Wem victorious over Rucrag and ended with House Wem victorious over Reswoumark, though the peace with Rucrag remained in place.

Kurnsa Bealldo took the seat of House Wem in 44 by election, following the killing of Lea Dae at Broldtemoor. In that same year, Mith Thres returned from exile to serve the house. The period ran for eight years, during which House Wem fought three battles, all won: against the Sworn Men of Rucrag at Rucrag in 48 with 178 dead, against the Sworn Men of Rucrag at Rucrag in 49 with 148 dead, and against the Second Crown of Reswoumark at Haelabarrow in 51 with 219 dead. Two wars were begun, lasting two years in total. House Wem sent one raid, to Rucrag in 47, which got through but took nothing. Five raids were suffered, all beaten off: by the Sworn Men of Rucrag at Broldtemoor in 44, by the Wougate Crown at Thu Pits in 45, by the Second Crown of Reswoumark at Wougate in 46, by the Wougate Crown at Wei Seam in 49, and by the Wougate Crown at Wougate in 50.

Kurnsa Bealldo ordered two killings against people of other powers: Threihoull Thild at Rucrag in 49 and Skove Pull at Rucrag in 50. Two of its own people were murdered from within by its own people. Two exiles returned to take service. The quarrel that kept returning was between House Wem and the Sworn Men of Rucrag, with two battles between them. Naerream Thrurei challenged Kurnsa Bealldo openly in 45, lost, and Kurnsa Bealldo kept the seat. The Wougate Crown and the Second Crown of Reswoumark each publicly slighted House Wem once.

Kurnsa Bealldo remained holding the seat when the period ended in 51.
